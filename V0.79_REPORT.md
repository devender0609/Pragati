## The sequencing recheck, and what it corrected

The verified Class 6 registry gives Chapter 3 as:

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| 3.1 | Numbers can Tell us Things | p. 55 |
| 3.2 | Supercells | p. 56 |
| 3.3 | Patterns of Numbers on the Number Line | p. 59 |
| 3.4 | Playing with Digits | p. 60 |

Starting at 3.1 holds. **The rest of my recommendation did not.**

I had said §3.1 and §3.2 should come first because "the existing lattice
and chapter artwork already draw number-grid and supercell reasoning".
That conflated two different systems. The chapter **artwork** draws a
decorative grid on the shelf tiles. The instructional `VisualSpec` union
supports exactly three things — number lines, fraction strips, fraction
area models — and there is no grid representation for teaching anything.

§3.1 does not need one: it is about reading meaning out of numbers in
context, and its representations are sentences and comparisons. So it is
authored, with `visuals: []` on purpose rather than by omission.

**§3.2 is not authored.** A supercell is a cell larger than its
neighbours; it is unteachable without a grid of numbers. It waits on a
`number_grid` spec and its renderer — a representation change, which
belongs in its own release rather than being smuggled into a content
one. Authoring §3.2 without it would have produced a lesson about a grid
that never shows a grid.

## What §3.1 contains

Learning goal, prior-knowledge check, vocabulary, six-step explanation,
three worked examples with reasoning on every step, three guided items,
five independent items, two reasoning tasks, a summary, a next step, and
full teacher notes. Sequence rules name the three representations it must
not introduce, each tied to the later section that owns it.

A new misconception registry, `numberPlayMisconceptions.ts`, holds four
records characteristic of this chapter — errors about comparison and
context rather than arithmetic. Two carry a `diagnosticSignal`, two
carry `null`, because a misconception that cannot be inferred from an
answer must be surfaced by teaching rather than guessed at. Choosing the
larger number is not evidence of "bigger is better" in most items, since
in most items the larger number is correct; only an item where more is
worse separates the two.

`sectionValidators` knew the Fractions registry by name, so a second
chapter could not reference its own misconceptions. Chapters now
register; the check is unchanged and still rejects an id that exists
nowhere.

## What this release does not claim

`reviewStatus: 'authored_draft'`. `competencyMappingStatus:
'competency_proposed'` — proposed by a maintainer, not reviewed.
Number Play's learn state is asserted by test to be **not**
`official_learn_available`: writing a lesson does not publish it, and
the student product still shows the chapter as being prepared.

The draft teaches the idea the section title names. It does **not**
reproduce the textbook's own activities or dialogue — Pragati has the
verified contents structure for Chapter 3, not a page-level reading of
its exercises. The teacher notes say exactly that and ask the teacher to
open the book's own opening activity alongside.
