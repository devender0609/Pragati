## What each destination got

**Overview.** Recomposed on the Teacher kit. The gradient "no student
work yet" banner and three numbered cards became one empty state whose
largest elements are the three actions, plus two panels of real fact:
what can be assigned today, and where the curriculum stands.

A defect fixed here: it computed `learnReady` from
`availability === 'available'`, which is the "Ready to learn" defect in
teacher language — a chapter whose only openable content is legacy
practice counted as a chapter with a lesson. Both counts now come from
`officialChapterLearnState` and are reported as the two different things
they are.

**Classes.** "Teacher dashboard" eyebrow removed; the rail already says
where you are. The row description promised "growth history" — Pragati
records sessions and responses and does not measure growth, so it now
says what it does. The empty roster is a setup step with one named
action instead of a decorative panel with a star in it.

**Assign.** Semantics from the previous pass preserved, and it now
renders inside the workspace rather than as a standalone route.

**Assess.** The two tracks are named before either is offered:
Instructional check (classroom evidence you interpret yourself) and
Pragati Growth (a governed instrument with calibration and norming
requirements, administered through its own path and never derived from a
check).

**Insights.** The empty analytical dashboard became an explanation of
what Insights will show plus the action that produces the evidence. It
promises no mastery, growth or proficiency.

**Resources.** Now a curriculum workspace that respects the reconciled
verification depth: Class 6 shows chapters and sections because a
contents page was read; Classes 9–12 show syllabus units and say plainly
that the textbook chapter denominator is not verified; unverified
classes say the denominator is unknown rather than showing zero.

**Navigation copy.** Insights read "Class-level growth, weak concepts,
and misconceptions." Pragati computes none of those. All five
descriptions were rewritten against what the screens do, and a test
fails on `class-level growth`, `weak concepts`, `mastery`,
`proficiency`, `ability scale`, `calibrated score` in any of them.
