## E. Representation — one general extension

Segments could already carry an **arrow** mark, the conventional way to *draw*
parallel lines. But a mark is ink: nothing stopped a figure putting arrows on
two segments that are not parallel, and BPT is a theorem whose entire
hypothesis is a parallel line. A proof figure that merely looked parallel
would have let the proof rest on the drawing.

So `parallelGroups` was added — a figure **asserts** parallelism and
`validateParallelGroups` recomputes directions from the coordinates by cross
product. It is a general geometry relation; nothing in it knows about
similarity. Tilting E by one unit in the BPT figure produces
*"claimed parallel but the coordinates make them cross"*.

All three semantic validators now run from the single `validateVisual` entry.

## F/G. The proof

`ncert_jemh1_s6_3_bpt_proof_lesson` proves BPT in full by the area route:
triangles sharing a height have areas in the ratio of their bases, giving
ar(ADE)/ar(BDE) = AD/DB and ar(ADE)/ar(DEC) = AE/EC, while the parallel makes
ar(BDE) = ar(DEC).

The third worked example asks **where the proof fails without the parallel**,
and names the single step. A hypothesis a student cannot locate in the
argument is one they will drop when applying it.

A test asserts the chain is present and would fail a lesson that said "it can
be shown".

## H. §6.4 — the central governance test

**No proof of any criterion appears.** `auditProofScope` enforces it, and the
hard part was building a check that is not a word ban:

- A §6.4 lesson **should** say *"prove that these two triangles are similar"*
  — that is applying the criterion, which is what the scope asks for.
- It **should** say *"we use this without proving it"* — an honest statement
  of scope.
- It **must not** prove the criterion.

The audit looks at what the proof verb takes as its object: a named criterion,
or a particular pair of triangles. Six fixtures — three that must fire, three
that must not — all behave correctly, and a negative test in the suite injects
*"We will now prove the AAA similarity criterion"* to confirm the auditor
bites.

Both lessons say openly that the proof is in the textbook and outside the
current curriculum. Students who notice the gap otherwise assume the lesson is
incomplete.

**State without proof is not state without understanding.** These lessons
teach what evidence satisfies each criterion, what does not, and how to choose
— they are not a table.

## I/J/K. Correspondence, criteria, invalid evidence

Vertex order is taught as carrying the matching, with a case where the same
two triangles make one statement true and another false. A test checks that
AB/PQ = BC/QR = CA/RP appears and that any wrong pairing is flagged rather
than used.

The **included angle** gets its own treatment, with a figure whose side
evidence is identical to the valid SAS example and only the angle's position
differs. That isolates the one thing being taught.

Two worked examples deliberately **end without an answer**, concluding the
evidence is insufficient. A test asserts the lessons distinguish *insufficient
evidence* from *not similar* — these are different claims, and conflating them
turns a gap in the data into a false statement about geometry.
