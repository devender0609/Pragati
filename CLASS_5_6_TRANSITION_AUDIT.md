# Class 5 to Class 6 — the primary-to-middle transition

Based on the complete Classes 1-5 decomposition and the two Class 6 chapters
decomposed so far (Number Play and Fractions). **[S]** marks a source
observation, **[P]** a Pragati recommendation.

## What changes at the boundary

**[S] The book itself changes shape.** Classes 1-5 (Math-Mela and its
predecessors) number no sections; Ganita Prakash numbers 65 sections across 10
chapters. The official record grain changes from chapter to section, and the
decomposition follows it.

**[S] Notation becomes load-bearing.** Class 5 fractions are read from strips,
circles and number lines. Class 6 names the *fractional unit*, writes mixed
numbers, converts between forms, reduces to lowest terms and adds unlike
fractions by a stated procedure (Brahmagupta's method). The representation is
still there, but the notation now carries the argument.

**[S] Reasoning demand rises sharply.** Number Play asks whether a statement is
always, sometimes or never true; whether an arrangement is possible at all;
what the winning strategy in a game is and why; and presents an unsolved
conjecture. Classes 1-5 carry reasoning-extension material in every book, but
always beside the teaching. In Class 6 it is the teaching.

## Strand by strand

**Fractions.** [S] Class 5 leaves students able to generate equivalent
fractions, compare by numerator, denominator or benchmark, and read fractions
greater than one. Class 6 assumes all of it and adds: fractional unit as a
named object, equal parts of unequal shape, fraction as measured length,
mixed-number conversion, simplest form, comparison by common denominator, and
fraction arithmetic. [S] **No prerequisite gap found** — every Class 6 fractions
unit rests on a Class 5 unit that exists. [P] Author in the source order;
the chapter is unusually well sequenced.

**Number.** [S] Class 5 reaches 100,000 with rounding. Class 6 Number Play
assumes five-digit fluency and spends the chapter on structure — digit counts,
digit sums, palindromes, the Kaprekar process, mental splitting at scale.
[S] Simplest form (7.6) needs factors and common factors, which Class 5
Chapter 13 introduces. That is the one cross-chapter dependency inside Class 6
that reaches back into Class 5.

**Possible duplicated scope.** [S] Palindromes appear in Class 5 Chapter 4 and
again in Class 6 §3.5; the Class 6 treatment turns them into an investigation
with an open question, so it is an extension rather than a repeat. [P] Author
them as one progression.

## Caveat

[S] Eight of the ten Class 6 chapters are not yet decomposed, so this audit
covers the number and fraction strands only. Geometry (Lines and Angles,
Constructions, Symmetry), data, primes, perimeter and area, and integers are
not yet assessed at the transition.
