# Class 5 to Class 6 — the primary-to-middle transition

Written after **both** classes reached source completion: Class 5's 15 chapters
and 200 pages, Class 6's 10 chapters, 65 numbered sections and 392 pages, every
picture-carried page rendered and looked at. **[S]** marks a source
observation, **[P]** a Pragati recommendation.

## What changes at the boundary

**[S] The book changes shape.** Math-Mela numbers no sections; Ganita Prakash
numbers 65 across 10 chapters. The official authoring record changes from
chapter to section, and the decomposition follows it.

**[S] Definition replaces description.** Class 5 describes a line, a corner, an
angle. Class 6 **defines** point, line segment, line, ray and angle, fixes the
naming conventions, and then tests figures against those definitions.

**[S] Measurement acquires units and instruments.** Class 5 measured turns with
a folded circle and no unit. Class 6 introduces the **degree** as one of 360
equal parts, the protractor, construction of a stated angle, and error analysis
of a misused instrument.

**[S] Justification becomes the task.** Class 6 asks *why* adding odd numbers
gives squares (with a picture that proves it), *why* the sieve works, *why* a
divisibility test works, *why* the smallest angle of symmetry divides 360. In
Classes 1-5 reasoning sat beside the teaching; here it is the teaching.

**[S] Tools arrive.** Ruler, compass and protractor are used as instruments of
construction, with planning from a rough diagram and verification afterwards.
Classes 1-5 drew on grids and by hand.

## Strand by strand

| Strand | Class 5 leaves | Class 6 assumes and adds |
|---|---|---|
| Patterns | growing patterns, tessellation | named sequences; **pictorial proof** of relations between them |
| Number structure | factors, multiples, divisibility by 2, 5, 10 | primes and composites, the sieve, co-primes, **prime factorisation**, justified divisibility tests |
| Fractions | equivalence, benchmarks, fractions > 1 | fractional unit named, mixed numbers, simplest form, comparison by common denominator, **fraction arithmetic** |
| Geometry / angles | angle as turn; quadrilateral families | point/segment/line/ray defined; **the degree**; protractor; reflex angles; angles on a straight line |
| Perimeter / area | area by counting; the first formula | general formulas; **area of a triangle**; area-versus-perimeter as a problem type |
| Construction | drawing on grids | **ruler-and-compass construction** from constraints; equidistant points |
| Symmetry | reflection and rotational symmetry noticed | **angles and order of symmetry in degrees**; the smallest angle divides 360 |
| Data | pictographs with scale, bar graphs | choosing a scale, **drawing rules**, judging whether a representation misleads |
| Negative numbers | — | **integers**: order below zero, two models, contexts, Brahmagupta's rules |

## Prerequisite gaps — checked across the whole book

**[S] One real dependency inside Class 6**: simplest form (§7.6) needs factors
and common factors, which Class 5 Chapter 13 supplies. Every other Class 6
unit rests on a Class 5 unit that exists.

**[S] One genuine discontinuity**: *The Other Side of Zero* has **no Class 1-5
precursor at all**. Negative numbers are not foreshadowed anywhere in the
primary stage — not on a number line, not in a context. This is the single
largest conceptual jump at the boundary, and it is a property of the source,
not a defect in the decomposition.

**[P]** Author the integer units with that in mind: the lift and token models
are doing the work a precursor would normally have done, so neither can be
abbreviated.

**[P]** The Class 6 progression relationships across all 75 units are
26 NEW_MATHEMATICAL_IDEA, 16 EXTENSION, 15 NEW_PROCEDURE, 14 FORMALIZATION, 3 NEW_REPRESENTATION, 1 INTEGRATION — the balance of NEW_MATHEMATICAL_IDEA and
FORMALIZATION is what makes this a stage boundary rather than another year.
