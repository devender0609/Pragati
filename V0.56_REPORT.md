## 3. The evidence gate — the critical fix

`frameworkEvidenceGate.ts`. v0.55's `mayFreezeFramework()` checked
reviewers and nothing else, so **two approving experts could have
frozen a framework whose two biggest structural questions rested on
documents nobody had opened.** Their approval would have been sincere
and uninformed, and the system would have recorded it as settled.

Three separate statuses now exist (evidence / human review / framework),
and `evaluateFrameworkGate()` requires **both** gates. Evidence is
evaluated first, so a fully-approved framework with unread sources
reports `blocked_by_evidence` — making the real blocker visible.

Required evidence is explicit, each entry stating *why* it is required.
PARAKH and NCF-SE are `pilot_freeze`-mandatory; not every conceivable
source is.

Three tests prove: perfect human agreement cannot bypass a failed
evidence gate; complete evidence cannot bypass human review; only both
together yield `approved_for_pilot`.

**One subtlety worth recording:** the gate keys on *source inspection*,
not on the matrix's evidence cells. Gating on the cells would have kept
decisions blocked even after a source was read and the cell populated —
the gate would never have opened.

## 6. Generation conflicts (§5)

New `requires_generation_review` state. On generation-sensitive claim
types, cross-generation conflicts are **no longer auto-resolved by
source ranking** — the record captures both claims, both generations,
and an `outcomeStatus` of retained / modified / moved / superseded /
unknown, defaulting to `unknown`. Same-generation conflicts still
resolve normally.

## 7. Progression claim taxonomy (§6)

`progression_order` is split into four types with genuinely different
authority orders:

| Claim | Strongest | Weakest |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `logical_prerequisite` | mathematical inference | secondary |
| `developmental_prerequisite` | expert judgement | secondary |
| `instructional_sequence` | current official curriculum | **mathematical inference** |
| `empirical_difficulty_progression` | expert judgement (data, later) | mathematical inference |

Mathematics ranks **last** for instructional sequence — a curriculum's
teaching order is a policy choice, and v0.55's single ordering let a
necessity argument override it.

## 13. Growth readiness architecture (§12)

`evaluateGrowthReadiness()` derives status from framework approval,
specification counts, review counts, field-test and calibration
evidence. It returns `not_eligible` for every chapter **because the
evidence says so, not because a constant says so** — and will advance on
its own when evidence changes. Tested: an unapproved framework blocks
even a fully calibrated bank.
