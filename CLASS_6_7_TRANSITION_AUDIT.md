# Class 6 to Class 7 — interim transition audit

Written from the complete Class 6 decomposition and the **8 Class 7 chapters**
completed so far: Large Numbers Around Us, Arithmetic Expressions, A Peek
Beyond the Point, Expressions Using Letter-Numbers, Parallel and Intersecting
Lines, Number Play, A Tale of Three Intersecting Lines, and Working with Fractions. **7 chapters remain unread**, so this audit is
deliberately partial. **[S]** source observation, **[P]** Pragati
recommendation.

## Number

**[S]** Class 6 reaches lakhs through place value; Class 7 opens at a lakh and
goes to crores and arabs, and to the Indian **and** American naming systems for
one number. Rounding becomes a choice of place rather than an instruction.
**[S]** No prerequisite gap in this strand: every Class 7 number unit rests on
Class 6 place value.

## Expressions — the sharpest change

**[S]** Classes 1-6 *compute* values. Class 7 makes the **expression itself the
object of study**: terms are named, subtraction is rewritten as adding a
negative, terms are swapped and grouped with the Class 6 token model supplying
the justification, brackets are removed under a stated rule, and the
distributive property is used as a tool.
**[P]** Author §2.1 and §2.2 in order and keep the token model visible; it is
the only bridge back to Class 6 the chapter offers.

## Decimals

**[S]** Class 7 subdivides the unit into tenths, then hundredths, then
thousandths, and only then names the decimal point — decimals arrive as an
**extension of place value**, not as new notation to memorise.
**[S]** §3.2 onward assumes the Class 6 fractional-unit work fluently; that
unit has an authored lesson, so the bridge exists.

## Algebra

**[S]** Letter-numbers are entirely new. Class 6 generalises patterns in words
and pictures; Class 7 writes 4n for the nth multiple of 4, collects like terms,
removes brackets in algebra and tests whether two expressions are equal by
substitution.
**[P]** Chapter 4 rests on chapter 2 — author them as one sequence.

## Geometry — a genuine jump

**[S]** **Parallelism and the transversal have no real Class 6 precursor beyond
angle measure.** Class 6 defines point, line, ray and angle, introduces the
degree and the protractor, and deduces angles on a straight line. Class 7 moves
to two lines and a third crossing them: corresponding angles become the *test*
for parallelism, and that test becomes a construction.
**[S]** Chapter 5 also brings the first multi-step geometric deduction in the
programme — chaining corresponding, alternate, vertically opposite and
linear-pair relations to find an unknown angle.
**[P]** Do not treat this as a continuation of Class 6 angle work; it is new
geometry and needs its own sequence.

## Number Play — parity as an argument

**[S]** Class 6 Number Play classifies and explores. Class 7 **uses parity as a
reasoning tool for impossibility**: two consecutive numbers cannot sum to 112,
and the parity of an algebraic expression settles what its values can be. The
same chapter uses letter-numbers to generalise a magic square, so chapter 4 is a
prerequisite for chapter 6.
**[S]** §6.1 deliberately revisits the Class 6 opening before extending it —
the one explicit REVISIT in the Class 7 units so far.

## Triangles — proof arrives

**[S]** Chapter 7 contains the programme's **first real geometric proof**: the
angles of a triangle sum to 180 degrees, argued from a parallel drawn through a
vertex using the transversal results of chapter 5. It also proves an existence
condition — the triangle inequality — from whether two circles intersect, and
then uses it to decide which side lengths are possible.
**[P]** Chapter 7 cannot be authored before chapter 5; the proof depends on it
directly.

## Fractions — not a repeat of Class 6

**[S]** Class 6 establishes fraction equivalence, comparison, and addition and
subtraction. **Class 7 adds multiplication and division**, both derived rather
than asserted: the unit-square area model gives the product rule, and division
is turned into multiplication by the reciprocal. It also asks a question Class 6
never does — whether a product is larger or smaller than the numbers multiplied.
**[P]** Treat Working with Fractions as new procedure plus new reasoning, not
as revision.
