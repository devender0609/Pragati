# Primary-stage progression audit — Classes 1 to 5

Written after all five classes reached source completion:
130 + 138 + 208 + 224 + 200 pages read in full text, every
picture-carried page rendered and looked at, 67 official chapters.

Each finding is marked **[S]** for a source observation or **[P]** for a
Pragati recommendation. Nothing here changes the official curriculum.

## Number and place value

**[S]** Counting to 100 (Class 1) → 999 with hundred as ten tens (Class 2) →
1000 with Dienes blocks, comparison with > and < (Class 3) → 10,000 with
regrouping in every place and four-digit comparison (Class 4) → 100,000 with
the TTh column, and **rounding** appearing for the first time (Class 5).
**[S]** The representation changes at each step: bundles → blocks → tokens →
arrow cards. **[P]** Author the place-value units in that order; each reuses
the previous representation before replacing it.

## Addition and subtraction

**[S]** Within 20 with strips (1) → two-digit with regrouping and fact
families (2) → three-digit with box diagrams and estimate-then-check (3) →
four-digit with tokens and quick ways (4) → five-digit with mental
compensation and the question "does this method always work?" (5).
**[S]** Class 5 adds a genuinely new idea: **sums of consecutive numbers**
found without adding, an early algebraic generalisation.

## Multiplication and division

**[S]** Equal groups (2) → tables from skip counting and stick arrays, sharing
with a repeated-subtraction column (3) → tables to 20, multiples of 10 and
100, division with a remainder (4) → doubling-and-halving, the grid method,
and **the division algorithm by place value** (5).
**[S]** Factors and primes are **new at Class 5** (Chapter 13); Class 4 had
multiples and common multiples only. **[P]** The Class 5 division units are
the natural first authoring target of the upper primary, because four earlier
classes feed them.

## Fractions

**[S]** Precursor in Class 2: sharing equally produces equal parts of a *set*,
never of one whole. Explicit introduction **Class 3 Chapter 8** (halves,
quarters of one whole). Extended Class 4 (unit fractions, non-unit fractions,
equivalence by folding). Formalized **Class 5 Chapter 2**: equivalence
generated systematically, comparison by numerator, by denominator and by
benchmarks, and **fractions greater than one** — which appear nowhere earlier.

## Measurement

**[S]** Direct comparison (1) → the *need* for a standard unit (2) → metre,
litre, kilogram with halves and quarters (3) → centimetre, m/cm conversion,
and **perimeter** (4) → kilometre, millimetre, milligram, quintal and tonne,
arithmetic on compound measures, and **the double number line for scaling**
(5). **[S]** **Area** is new at Class 5 Chapter 11, and the area formula is
the first formula of the primary stage. **[S]** A real gap worth noting:
perimeter is taught at Class 4 and area at Class 5, so the two are compared
only once, in Class 5 Chapter 11. **[P]** Author that comparison unit
carefully; it carries the whole relationship.

## Geometry, angles and symmetry

**[S]** Long/round and rolls/slides (1) → 2-D shapes, faces, edges (2) →
square corners, tiling, reflection symmetry (3) → prisms, pyramids, nets,
acute/right/obtuse angles, circles with radius and diameter (4) → **angle as
turn with a measuring tool**, quadrilateral families, tessellation with
regular polygons, and **rotational symmetry** (5).
**[S]** Angle is met twice from different directions: as two lines meeting
(Class 4) and as an amount of turn (Class 5). **[P]** Author the Class 5 turn
unit as a bridge rather than a repeat; the two definitions must be joined
explicitly or degrees will arrive without ground.

## Data, time, money and position

**[S]** Picture counts (1) → pictographs, calendar, clock, coins (2) →
calendar arithmetic, elapsed minutes, notes to `500 (3) → leap years, AM/PM
and the 24-hour clock, deposit slips, tally-to-pictograph (4) → **seconds**,
hour-minute conversion, **scaled pictographs and the bar graph**, and **grid
coordinates** (5).
**[S]** Money stops developing after Class 4; Class 5 uses it only inside
other problems. **[P]** Not a defect in the source, but worth noting before
Class 6 mapping.

## Reasoning and problem solving

**[S]** Every class from 1 to 5 carries reasoning-extension material —
constraint grids, invariant tricks, deduction puzzles, winning strategies —
consistently outside what its chapters teach. 18 units across the five
classes are flagged for the single policy decision
`reasoning_extension_core_vs_enrichment`.
**[P]** Decide that policy once, as policy, before authoring begins.

## Possible duplicate scope

**[S]** Reflection symmetry is taught in Classes 3, 4 and 5. The Class 5
treatment is a deliberate revisit that grounds rotational symmetry, and the
Class 3 and 4 treatments differ in representation (rangoli and mirrors versus
tiles and mirror-writing). **[P]** Author them as one progression rather than
three parallel lessons.
