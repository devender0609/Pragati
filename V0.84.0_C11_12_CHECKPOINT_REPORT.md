## C/D. Architecture — verdict **B**

`complexNumbers.ts` holds one exact object that drives every downstream form.

**Exactness.** Real and imaginary parts are exact rationals, so `1/3 + i/7` is
a number rather than an approximation of one. The modulus reuses Class 10's
`Exact` — the `(p/q)√k` helper that already existed — so `|3+4i|` is `5` and
`|1+i|` is `√2`. No second radical system was invented.

**The argument is a typed `Angle`** from checkpoint 80's `angleMeasure`, not a
bare number that has forgotten whether it means degrees or radians.

**Refusals, not fudges.** Division by zero throws. Zero has no argument, and
`polarForm` reports that rather than quietly returning 0. Powers of `i` reduce
by remainder modulo 4, so `i²⁰²⁶` is one division rather than 2026
multiplications and no floating complex exponentiation appears anywhere.

**Identity is separate from display.** `3 + (-2)i` renders as `3 − 2i`,
`5 + 0i` as `5`, `0 + 1i` as `i`. Equality compares parts, never text.

## F/G/H. Arithmetic, powers, division

Addition and subtraction work componentwise. Multiplication gets its minus
sign from `i²` appearing, not from a remembered rule, and a test asserts the
parts-times-parts error is not what `multiply` does. The conjugate makes the
division denominator real (`c² + d²`), and `multiply(divide(z,w), w)` returns
`z` exactly.

## I. Modulus and conjugate

Exact throughout. The source-supported invariants hold on every canonical
number: conjugating twice returns `z`; `z` times its conjugate is real,
non-negative and equals `|z|²`; `z` plus its conjugate is twice the real part;
`|z|` is zero only for zero; a number and its conjugate share a modulus.

## J. The §4.3.6 radical caution

Preserved structurally. The model offers **no operation** that could perform
`√(−a)·√(−b) = √(ab)` — the safe route is `i` out first, giving `−6` where the
unsafe route would give `6`. Every complex root is written with `i` outside
the radical, and a test asserts no negative radicand ever appears.

## AE. Next

The remaining frontier has thinned. Sets, functions, trigonometry and complex
numbers between them now cover set semantics, function-as-object semantics,
exact angles and exact arithmetic with a plane representation.

**Conic Sections** is the strongest next candidate. It reuses the coordinate
and exact-radical machinery, and its eight units share one semantic frame —
a locus condition becoming an equation — which is the pattern that made Sets
and Complex Numbers tractable. **Permutations and Combinations** is the other
option, needing counting semantics that nothing existing provides, which makes
it a genuinely new frontier rather than a reuse win.

Carry forward from this checkpoint: the four-layer visual rule held, and it
should hold again for any conic locus figure.
