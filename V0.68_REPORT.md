## The eighteen questions

### 1. Did the misconception model become chapter-wide?

**Yes.** A misconception is now a *reference*, not an enum value:

```ts
{ source: 'section_7_4_map',            id: MisconceptionId }
{ source: 'fractions_chapter_registry', id: FractionsMisconceptionId }
```

`misconceptionResolver.ts` reads both through one interface. `judge()` returns
`misconceptionId` (legacy, unchanged), `chapterMisconceptionId` (new) and
`misconceptionRef` (unified).

The mechanism that made this possible without touching the fingerprint: the
§7.4 map moved **byte-for-byte** into `section74Misconceptions.ts` (so the
resolver has no import cycle) and is re-exported from its old home, while
`Choice` gained an **optional** `chapterMisconceptionId`. §7.4's choices never
set it, so `JSON.stringify` omits the key entirely and the serialised payload
does not move. A test asserts this rather than the comment claiming it.

Feedback text is never duplicated onto items. One record per misconception,
resolved at judging time.

### 4. How many remain neutral because inference is unsafe?

**9 of 18 incorrect options.** Neutral is not a defect — a chapter where every
wrong answer is diagnosed has stopped admitting that some are unknowable. A test
asserts the neutral count stays above zero.

Examples of deliberate refusals:

- **§7.8 option `1/8`** — could be halving the answer, a mis-ordered addition,
  or a slip. Three routes, no diagnosis.
- **§7.6 option "They are not the same amount"** — matches
  `equivalent_means_identical_picture`, but a student may simply not have worked
  it out. The option gives no reason, so no reason may be inferred.
- **`parts_count_vs_part_size`** is marked structurally undiagnosable, and
  `assertDiagnosable()` now *throws* if anyone tries to attach it.

An equivalent form of a diagnostic value is explicitly not the same evidence:
entering `3/2` for 2¾ gets neutral guidance even though `6/4` gets diagnosed.

### 5. Which sections gained interactions, and why?

Four, each answering §5's actual question — *does an interactive response
evidence something static practice cannot?*

| Section | Format | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **7.1** | `area_model_selection` | Guided item s71.g3 asks whether three unequal ribbon pieces are thirds and accepts "No". A student can answer "No" having learnt only that ribbon questions are trick questions. Selecting the correct partition requires reading the partition. |
| **7.2** | `area_model_selection` | Every static item runs diagram→fraction. Running it fraction→diagram fails differently: a student who memorised "top number, count the shaded" cannot reverse it without the meaning. |
| **7.3** | `fraction_strip_selection` | Static items convert "5 quarter-units" into 5/4 — arithmetic on words. Picking the strip that reaches the length makes the student read a quantity off a picture. Deliberately *not* a number line: that belongs to §7.4. |
| **7.5** | `numeric_entry` + number line | The chapter documented `mixed_number_is_multiplication` and never tested for it anywhere. |

Interactive items: 16 → **21**.

### 6. Which sections intentionally remain non-interactive?

**§7.9 only.** The learning there is discussion — that notation was invented by
people solving problems and could have been otherwise. A multiple-choice quiz on
names and dates would assess recall of facts the section never asks anyone to
memorise. The reasoning task (discovered vs invented) is the right instrument
and already exists.

Every other section now has at least one, so counts were never evened out — they
converged because each section independently answered yes.

### §6 — one new format, three rejected

`area_model_selection` was added because a strip is one-dimensional and
partitioned equally *by construction*: it literally cannot render an unequal
partition, which is the exact thing §7.1 and §7.2 turn on.

Rejected, with reasons recorded so they are not re-proposed forever:

- **`mixed_fraction_match`** — free numeric entry diagnoses the target
  misconception *better*. A student who types 6/4 has **produced** the
  multiplication error; one who drags 2¾ onto 6/4 has **recognised** it among
  options offered to them. Recognition is weaker evidence.
- **`equivalence_selection`** — strip selection already asks exactly this.
- **`comparison_selection`** — reasoned multiple choice carries more, because
  the option states *why*.
- **`drag_and_drop_ordering`** — would demo well and evidence nothing extra.

### 7. How many of the 31 worked examples were revised?

**2 revised, 29 accepted as is, 0 removed.**

**s71.we1 — the guava example.** Premise was *"Three guavas together weigh 1 kg.
They are about the same size"*, then divided by three to get exactly 1/3 kg.
Similar size does not give equal weight. §7.1 exists to establish that parts
must be **exactly** equal, so the example contradicted the section it belongs
to, and taught that an eyeballed resemblance licenses an exact fractional model.
Rewritten to a stated equal division; the hedging "about" is gone from the
answer.

**s72.we3 — the same-whole condition.** Asked whether 3 of 4 sectors of a circle
is *"the same amount as"* 3 of 4 parts of a rectangle. If the shapes differ in
size the answer is no. The stored answer covered itself with "of their own
whole" while the prompt still said "the same amount" — the condition was dodged
rather than taught. The prompt now asks what fraction each shows, and a fourth
step states explicitly that this does not make the two coloured amounts equal.

### 8. How many of the 69 practice items were revised or removed?

**3 revised, 66 accepted as is, 0 removed.** (26 guided + 43 independent.)

- **s73.i5** — asked a §7.3 student to compare 3/4 with 5/8, requiring
  equivalent fractions (§7.6) and unlike comparison (§7.7). Replaced.
- **s75.i4** — item in scope, but its rationale justified the answer by
  comparing 1/4 with 1/2 (§7.7). Rewritten to stay in fourths.
- **s76.i2** — rationale read *"Ten halves-of-fifths: 5 of 10 covers one half."*
  The first clause is not a phrase that means anything. Rewritten.

Item quantity was not inflated. Two distractors were also repaired (see Q12).

### 9. How many reasoning tasks were revised?

**1 of 13.**

**s77.r1** had three defects at once: an ungrammatical prompt (*"explain how you
know 5/9 is less than 6/11 is FALSE or TRUE"*); a self-contradiction — it forbade
a common denominator and the expected reasoning then used one (55/99 vs 54/99);
and it reasoned with **4.5 and 5.5**, decimals, which the current Grade 6 book
does not contain at all, since Ganita Prakash has no decimals chapter. Replaced
with *"Is 4/9 or 5/8 closer to one half?"*, which the landmark method actually
settles. A test asserts no decimal appears in that task.

### 10. What reading/readability concerns were found?

The ordinary stem validator is unchanged and green: **90 within standard, 0
rewrite required.**

The new advisory audit covers **414 prose pieces** the stem validator never saw
— learning goals, explanations, worked-example steps and reasoning, feedback,
vocabulary and summaries. Two long sentences were found and split (s73
explanation[5] at 33 words carrying the section's entire conceptual move from
area to length; s76 explanation[4] at 30 words holding the equivalence argument).

**1 flag remains, in §7.7**, and it was reviewed and kept: the summary carries
three tracked terms in 17 words, but it is the summary of a section about
denominators and numerators — removing the words would remove the summary. It is
recorded in the audit log so the flag is answered rather than ignored.

One threshold was recalibrated during the work. The first run flagged a
four-word reasoning step reading "Same denominator." as jargon-dense at 25 terms
per hundred words. Arithmetically true, pedagogically meaningless — a ratio needs
a denominator big enough to mean something, which is the same lesson this chapter
teaches. A 15-word minimum now applies to that flag.

**No readability grade is claimed.** Flesch-Kincaid and its relatives are
validated on American English prose and rest on syllable counting; this content
is Indian English for multilingual Grade 6 readers and is full of notation
("3/8", "2¾") that every syllable counter mis-parses. Producing "reading age
11.4" from that machinery would be a number with no evidence behind it. Every
output is a count or a flag, and a test asserts the report contains no
readability formula name and no reading age.

### 11. What sequence-boundary issues were found?

**Two real violations**, both listed above (s73.i5, s75.i4).

Both matter for a structural reason: **neither would ever have failed the keyword
validator**, because the offence is in what the item *requires*, not what it
says. Neither names "number line", "common denominator" or any other forbidden
phrase.

`fractionsBoundaries.ts` now records MAY ASSUME / TEACH HERE / DEFER TO for all
nine sections from the verified textbook sequence, and `checkBoundaries()`
compares it against each section's own declaration — two independent statements
of one plan, so a disagreement surfaces. The keyword `sequenceLeaks()` guardrail
is retained and still green.

**This does not prove sequence fidelity.** Catching what an item *requires* took
a human reading all 69 items, and no automation in this build replaces that.

One deliberate keep: §7.9 mentions that Indian mathematicians set out rules for
multiplication and division of fractions — operations deferred to Grade 7. It is
a historical statement about what was written down, not instruction in the
operations, and removing it would misreport the source. Flagged in the audit log
so a reviewer can overrule the judgement.

### 12. What mathematical errors were found in manual audit?

**Seven, beyond v0.67's two.** The five not already covered above:

- **s78.p2 option d** read *"1/2 is impossible here"* — mathematically **false**.
  1/2 *is* the answer in lowest terms, so a student picking it might have been
  right for the wrong reason and would then be told they were wrong. Replaced
  with 4/4.
- **s77.r1** used decimals that do not exist in the Grade 6 book (Q9).
- **s76.p1** offered 5/9 against a target of 2/3 — an unrelated wrong number
  testing nothing. Changed to 2/9, which keeps the target numerator and varies
  only the denominator.
- **s76.i2** rationale was meaningless prose.
- **s71.we1 / s72.we3** — the two modelling defects in Q7.

14 audit entries are recorded in `contentAuditLog.ts` with finding, action and
governing clause. `accepted_as_is` counts are **derived** from the live chapter
(total minus everything else), so they cannot drift when content changes.

Two iterations of hand audit found nine errors between them. That is the honest
reason to expect a real reviewer to find more.

### 17. Is the short per-section review instrument ready as a DRAFT?

**Yes — 10 questions, `status: 'draft_not_activated'`, and
`instrumentMayBeIssued()` returns false.**

Covers mathematical accuracy, textbook fidelity, sequence, explanation quality,
visual correctness, practice quality, misconception/feedback quality,
age/readability, teacher usefulness, and an overall revision severity. ~20
minutes per section.

Each question maps to a defect class this chapter has actually produced, not to a
category that sounded thorough. Q7 (*"Could a student give this answer for a
different reason?"*) is the question v0.67 got wrong twice.

**Not to be issued** until the §7.4 Package B review is adjudicated — that review
tests the template all nine sections follow, so sending eight more questionnaires
now would collect the same objection eight times. 37 × 9 = 333 questions is not a
request any teacher should receive.

### 18. What should v0.69 do?

**If §7.4 feedback has arrived:** adjudicate it. Classify every comment before
changing anything, apply section-specific comments to §7.4 alone, and re-read
each section individually for template-wide ones. If §7.4 content changes,
create artifact **v2** — do not overwrite v1.

**If it has not:** the highest-value work is not more code. It is sending the
review. Concretely:

1. **Send Package A/B for §7.4.** Everything downstream is blocked on it.
2. **Verify one of Classes 1–5, 7 or 8 by hand.** Fifteen minutes with a browser
   and a Contents page converts a null into a real denominator. Class 7 is the
   highest value: two Pragati modules claim alignment to it and secondary
   sources actively disagree about the chapter count.
3. **Consider whether five documented-but-never-diagnosed misconceptions should
   get diagnostic distractors** — or whether their sections genuinely cannot
   support a safe inference. Either answer is fine; the current silence is not.

Do **not** start another chapter, and do not build another governance layer.

## Classes 1–12 curriculum completeness

Full detail in `CLASSES_1_12_CURRICULUM_COMPLETENESS_AUDIT.md`.

**Headline: 5 of 12 grades have a primary-verified official structure (Classes
6, 9, 10, 11, 12), covering 34 official units. The remaining 7 have an unknown
number of official units — not zero.**

**Newly verified this iteration:** Classes IX–XII, read directly from the CBSE
Academic Unit's 2026-27 Mathematics curriculum documents on 2026-08-27 (6, 7, 5
and 6 units respectively, with full topic lists and marks weighting).

**Not verified, deliberately:** Classes 1–5, 7, 8. `ncert.nic.in` disallows
automated access and no attempt was made to circumvent it. Textbook titles are
corroborated by secondary sources; chapter lists are **null**, not zero and not
inherited from the pre-NCF books. Each pending record carries the exact manual
step needed to close the gap.

Official curriculum and Pragati coverage are now permanently separate.
`checkRegistryInvariants()` enforces four properties: unknown is never rendered
as zero, legacy module counts never determine official counts, coverage never
exceeds the official structure, and a verified grade always exposes every unit
title. Class 10 correctly shows **7 official chapters with 0 Pragati content** —
the chapters no longer disappear.

Pragati represents 1 of the 34 known official units. That ratio is the honest
state of the product; the registry exists to make it visible, not to be filled
in. A test asserts no placeholder lessons were created to close it.

## What must not be claimed from this build

- No section is educator-reviewed. Nothing is publishable.
- The boundary table and the readability audit are **guardrails, not proof**.
  Both of this iteration's real sequence violations were invisible to
  automation and took a human reading every item.
- The readability report is structural counts. It is not a reading level.
- 9 diagnostic distractors means 9 *claims* that a response admits one reading.
  Each `whyUnique` argument is a human judgement a reviewer may reject — two
  such arguments were rejected in v0.67, which is why the audit exists.
- Classes 9–12 topic lists come from the CBSE syllabus, authoritative for
  assessment but not the textbook's own contents page.
- Growth is untouched: no changes to CAT, scoring, norms, secure items,
  calibration or mastery claims. All Growth tests preserved.
