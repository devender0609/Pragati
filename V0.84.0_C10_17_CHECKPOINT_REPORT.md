## Three false positives — all mine

Worth recording, because the pattern is the finding.

**The proof detector.** A first version looked for the literal labels `GIVEN`
and `TO PROVE` and flagged **11 artifacts**. Every one was a false positive.
Only checkpoint 67 adopted those labels; the proofs written in checkpoints
56, 57, 58 and 62 state their hypotheses in prose — "In triangle ABC let DE
be parallel to BC" is a statement of givens however it is typeset. The check
is now semantic.

**The expectation map.** A section-level map marked every artifact in §8.2 as
PROVE because one unit in that section is. Replaced with a per-artifact map.

**The scope check.** My replacement then flagged `s6_4_aaa_lesson` for
setting out a proof. The lesson says in its own learning goal that AAA is
"taken as given without proving it", and its "show that these triangles are
similar" is an *application* of the criterion. **Checkpoint 57 had already
built `auditProofScope` to make exactly that distinction, and mine was
worse.** The auditor now delegates to it.

The lesson for later waves: before writing a new audit, check whether the
distinction it needs already exists.

### Finding 1 — MINOR, presentation: two proof conventions coexist

Three artifacts use labelled `GIVEN` / `TO PROVE`; fifteen state hypotheses
in prose. Both are mathematically sound and the prose ones identify their
hypotheses clearly — the BPT proof even names the single step where the
parallel is used. But a student meeting both forms sees two different-looking
things called a proof. **Not repaired**: this is a house-style decision for a
human, and changing fifteen lessons on my own judgement would version them
all. Pinned by test so it cannot drift further unnoticed.

### Finding 2 — OBSERVATION, pedagogy: a lesson tells students to disregard their textbook

`s1_2_fta_lesson` says the printed introduction announces a divisibility
method no longer in the course, "so ignore that sentence." This is the
honest handling of rationalisation residue and the alternative — silence —
would leave students confused by their own book. But instructing a student to
disregard part of the prescribed textbook is a policy judgement above an
authoring decision. **Flagged for human review, not repaired.**
