## E/F. Architecture — verdict B

The existing coordinate infrastructure is **equation-driven**:
`LinearSystemGraphSpec` derives its geometry from equations, and
`PolynomialGraphSpec` samples a function. Neither can represent a *point* as
a first-class object, and nothing in the codebase had a segment, a distance
or a dividing point.

`coordinateGeometry.ts` adds that: `Point` with exact rational coordinates,
`distanceSquared`, `distance` as an exact radical, `sectionPoint`,
`midpoint`, `ratioOf`, `isInternalPoint`, `collinearByDistance`,
`classifyQuadrilateral`, `isEquidistant`, plus validators. Reusable for any
coordinate work, not Chapter 7 specific.

## H. Distance derivation

Derived from the right triangle, with the right angle justified **from the
axes being perpendicular** rather than from the drawing — so the derivation
holds across quadrants. The sign point is made from the squaring itself
rather than by introducing absolute values as a correction, and only the
non-negative root is taken.

Distances stay **exact radicals**: 3√2, not 4.24, with the decimal offered
once and labelled approximate. Symmetry is asserted over every ordered pair
of canonical points.

## I. Distance applications

**Collinearity** returns the point *between*, not merely a yes. Which sum
holds names the middle point, and the lessons treat that as part of the
answer.

**Quadrilaterals** are classified from all four sides *and* both diagonals. A
rhombus with four equal sides and unequal diagonals sits beside the square in
the same lesson, so equal sides are visibly not sufficient. All four kinds —
square, rectangle, rhombus, parallelogram — classify correctly.

**Equidistance** squares both sides at the start, and the answer (−7, 0) is
verified back: both squared distances are 106.

## J/K. Section formula and the ratio convention

Derived from **similar triangles** formed by projecting onto the axes, with
similarity established from parallel horizontals rather than appearance. The
cross-multiplication is where the opposite weighting becomes visible.

The convention is stated, not memorised: AP : PB = m₁ : m₂, and m₁ multiplies
the **far** endpoint because a long AP puts P near B. The lessons teach a
position check — ask which endpoint the answer should be near — and
`ratioOf` recovers the ratio independently as the stronger check.

Reversal is tested at three ratios: the two orders give different points, and
`validateSection` rejects the wrong one.

## L. Midpoint regression

`midpoint` *is* `sectionPoint(a, b, 1, 1)`, not a second formula. A test
compares it against the Class 9 average form for **every ordered pair of
canonical points** and requires exact rational equality. The lesson derives
it rather than restating it, and notes that 1 : 1 is the one ratio where
reversing the weights is harmless.

## M. Internal division

`sectionPoint` throws on a non-positive ratio part, so external division
cannot be expressed. `isInternalPoint` requires the parameter strictly
between 0 and 1 — an endpoint fails, and a point beyond B on the same line
fails. Seven ratios are checked internal.

## N. Cross-dimension reconciliation

`ratioOf` solves from both coordinates and returns **null** when they
disagree, so an authored point that is off the segment cannot be given a
ratio. The degenerate case is handled honestly: on a vertical or horizontal
segment one coordinate is uninformative rather than contradictory, and the
other decides alone — both tested.
