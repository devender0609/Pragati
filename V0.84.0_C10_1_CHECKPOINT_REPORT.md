## D. Representation audit — what existed, what did not

## E. Architecture change — a general extension, not a trig type

Added to the existing `GeometryFigureSpec`:

- `rightTriangleRoles` — reference vertex, right-angle vertex, third vertex.
- `exactGeometry` — whether the drawn coordinates are meant to be exact.

No `TrigonometryFigureSpec` was created. These are **right-triangle**
semantics, not trigonometric ones; a Pythagoras lesson can say which side its
argument concerns using the same field.

## F. The semantic model

The roles hang off the **reference vertex**, and a figure may declare several
sets for one triangle. That is the mathematical fact: the same segment is
opposite angle A and adjacent to angle B. Recording "opposite" as a property
of a side would record a falsehood the moment the reference angle changes.

Only the hypotenuse is absolute, because the right angle fixes it — and the
model reflects that by deriving it rather than storing it.

`validateRightTriangleRoles` recomputes from coordinates: exactly one right
angle, hypotenuse not touching the right-angle vertex, reference angle acute,
opposite side not touching the reference vertex, adjacent leg meeting both,
adjacent ≠ hypotenuse, non-degenerate, every named side actually drawn.

`validateAngleMarkGeometry` checks a claimed 30/45/60/90 against the actual
coordinates wherever `exactGeometry` is set. A derivation that reads 60
degrees off a picture drawn at 57 teaches the student to trust the picture.

## H. Proof fidelity — 8.2b

The lesson **opens the gap before closing it**: sin A was defined as opposite
over hypotenuse, but *which* triangle? Infinitely many contain the angle, and
nothing so far says they agree.

The proof then runs the full chain — two right triangles sharing the acute
angle, equal angles at A and equal right angles, **AA**, the correspondence
fixed by the right-angled vertices (B to P, not to Q), proportional
corresponding sides, and the rearrangement from `BC/PQ = AC/AQ` to
`BC/AC = PQ/AQ` that turns a between-triangles statement into a within-triangle
one.

It says explicitly why measuring two triangles would not be a proof.

## I. Special angles — 8.3a

Derived, not tabulated. 45 from the isosceles right triangle with legs 1 and
hypotenuse √2. 30 and 60 together from an equilateral triangle of side 2 cut
by its altitude, giving 1, √3, 2 — with the altitude's status as median and
bisector stated as the fact being used.

sin 30 = cos 60 is **read off the shared triangle**, as the role-swap from the
first lesson, rather than offered as a rule.

## J. Mathematical validation

`STANDARD_ANGLE_RATIOS` is a single structured source of truth. A test
recomputes every entry from the derivation triangles, checks tan = sin/cos,
checks every acute sine and cosine lies strictly in (0,1), and asserts the
prose states the same values. A retyped table with one wrong cell is invisible
to a reader and fatal to everything after it.

Exact forms only — √3/2, never 0.866, enforced by test. A further test scans
all four lessons for any sine or cosine claimed above 1.
