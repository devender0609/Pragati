## C. The architecture boundary — verdict **B**

**Class 10 was not touched.** `specialAngles` and `rightTriangleRoles` are
byte-identical, and a test asserts their table still reads `1/√2`, `1/2` and
`not defined` exactly as before.

Two new modules sit beside them.

**`angleMeasure.ts`** makes the unit part of the angle. There is no
representation of an angle that has forgotten what it measures, and
`sameAngle(degrees(60), piMultiple(60))` is **false** — 60 degrees and 60
radians are different angles and both are expressible. Degree angles are
exact rationals; radian angles are exact rational multiples of π, so a radian
that is not such a multiple is **not representable rather than approximated**.
Conversion is exact and sign-preserving: `-45°` → `−π/4`.

**`circularFunctions.ts`** reads cos and sin off unit-circle coordinates and
**derives the other four by division**, so a zero denominator produces an
undefined value instead of a number needing annotation. `tan 90°` is *not
defined*; `numberOf` throws rather than returning Infinity, and a test checks
every defined value is finite and not NaN.

Exact arithmetic is borrowed from Class 10's `Exact`, which is `(p/q)√k` with
no acute assumption in it — reuse of a number helper, not of the trigonometry.

## F. Class 10 compatibility — 18/18, by value

All six ratios at 30°, 45° and 60° agree **to exact value**, zero mismatches.

Six of the eighteen differ in **written convention**: Class 10 leaves `1/√3`,
Class 11 rationalises to `√3/3`. Both correct. The comparison is by value
deliberately — comparing strings would have forced one model to adopt the
other's typography, which is precisely the distortion this checkpoint was
meant to avoid.

A test also confirms the Class 10 model is **never asked about an argument
outside its domain**: `ratioAt(150)` still throws, and the Class 11 model
answers there instead.

## D/E/G. Angles, unit circle, quadrants

Coterminal angles are recognised without being equated: `30°` and `390°` are
coterminal and **not** the same angle. All 16 multiples of 30° and 45° lie on
the unit circle and satisfy the Pythagorean identity.

Quadrant signs are **computed from coordinates**, not stored. Every
reciprocal's sign equals its partner's, by test. An axis argument is reported
as `positive_y` or `negative_x` rather than being forced into a quadrant —
the case a mnemonic cannot express.

## H/I. Domain, range, period, graphs

Period verified by sampling for all six: 2π for sin, cos, sec, cosec; π for
tan and cot. Range respected at every sampled argument.

**Graphs are lists of separate branches.** The tan graph has 3 branches and 2
breaks over one period; sin has 1 branch and 0 breaks. Every plotted point is
regenerated from the function and compared. A deliberately joined graph is
**rejected** with "crosses the undefined argument" — the break is in the data,
not in the renderer's good manners.

## J/K. Identities

`cos(x − y)` is derived from the chord between two unit-circle points; every
other identity follows by substitution, and each lesson records which parent
it came from. The multiple-angle lesson teaches the dependency chain rather
than a formula sheet, and its Think deeper asks what is lost by listing them.

The checkpoint-77 correction is preserved: that unit points at **§3.4**, not
the nonexistent 3.4.1.

Numeric checks appear only as error-detection — one lesson states plainly that
finitely many agreements cannot establish an identity.

## O. General solutions

Parameterised over **ℤ**, not the naturals, and verified at n = −3…3 for all
three equations. Tangent uses `nπ` and cosine `2nπ`, matching their periods.
A single value is treated as an incomplete answer, which is the error the
lesson exists to correct.

## P. Defects found and fixed

**A division bug in my own model.** `divide` kept a factor that cancelled,
making `tan 45° = 2` and `tan 30° = √3`. **The Class 10 compatibility check
caught it** — which is what that check is for.

**A provenance gap from checkpoint 79.** The `cbse_only` kind was added but
`officialSectionId` was left required, invisible until the first such lesson
existed. Rather than widening the type across 13 call sites, I followed the
precedent `source_segment` already set: the field is empty and the validator
requires it empty, so no fabricated id can slip in.

**A real subsection gap.** Three lessons name subsections 3.2.1, 3.3.1 and
3.3.2, and the official-section registry holds **sections only** — the 67
subsections live in the frozen source model and were never registered. Added
`officialSubsectionNumber`: `officialSectionId` names the registered parent
and the subsection is recorded beside it. Both true, neither invented.

**A chapter-ordering case.** FO3 has no textbook page, so ordering by page put
it first. It is now placed last by instructional sequence and excluded from
the page-order check, rather than being given a page it does not have.

Five baselines moved for Class 11 reaching 20 lessons. The master map reports
**12 sections** against **20 lessons** — both right, counting different things.

## AB. Next

The risk map has moved. Sets and trigonometry were the two largest C-class
frontiers and both are now built, so the question is which remaining chapter
reuses the most.

**Chapter 2, Relations and Functions**, is the strongest candidate. It sits
directly on `setTheory` — a relation is a subset of a Cartesian product — and
it needs the function-as-object semantics that `circularFunctions` has now
prototyped in a special case: domain, range, period and evaluation already
exist there for six functions, and generalising them is a smaller step than
inventing them. It would also close the composition gap FO-2 leaves open.

**Chapter 4, Complex Numbers**, is the genuinely new frontier and reuses
almost nothing, which argues for doing it later rather than sooner.
