# Classes 1 to 6 — the primary stage and its boundary

Written from the complete decomposition of six classes: 1292 pages read in
full text across 77 official chapters, every picture-carried page rendered and
looked at. This is not the earlier reports concatenated: it asks what changes
in *kind* when the primary stage ends. **[S]** source observation, **[P]**
Pragati recommendation.

## Abstraction

**[S]** Classes 1-5 keep every idea attached to a referent: a bundle of ten, a
paratha cut in four, a metre of rope. Class 6 detaches them. A point "must be
imagined to be invisibly thin". A fractional unit is an object with a name. A
negative number is a floor, then a token, then a rule. **[P]** Author the
Class 6 units so the model is visibly discarded at the end, not quietly kept.

## Notation

**[S]** Notation is descriptive through Class 5 — numerals, +, -, ×, ÷, the
fraction bar. At Class 6 it becomes **load-bearing**: angle ABC cannot be
called angle B when three rays meet there; +3 and -3 are different objects;
1/3 and 4/12 must be recognised as one number written two ways.

## Representation

**[S]** Classes 1-3 use pictures as illustration. Classes 4-5 use them as
measurement (grids, number lines, double number lines). **Class 6 uses a
picture as a proof** — the square of dots that shows why consecutive odd
numbers sum to a square. That is a change of role, not of quality.

## Reasoning

**[S]** The programme carries reasoning-extension material in every class, but
always beside the chapter. At Class 6 the chapter itself asks for
justification: why 1 is neither prime nor composite, why the folded creases are
exactly right angles, why the smallest angle of symmetry divides 360, whether
every 2-digit start reaches a palindrome (unknown), whether Collatz terminates
(unsolved). **[S]** The programme first says "we do not know" at Class 6.

## Procedure

**[S]** Procedures become stated and general: Brahmagupta's method for adding
unlike fractions, the division algorithm (Class 5) extended, the divisibility
tests with their justifications, the construction sequences. **[P]** Each
Class 6 procedure should be authored with the argument attached; the source
always gives one.

## Tool use

**[S]** New at Class 6: protractor, compass, and the discipline of planning a
construction on a rough diagram before drawing it. **[P]** These need
authoring as skills in their own right; no Class 1-5 unit prepares them.

## Multi-step application

**[S]** Class 5 problems are mostly two-step. Class 6 routinely chains:
construct, then verify against properties; factorise, then decide
co-primality; measure, then classify, then deduce the unknown angle.
