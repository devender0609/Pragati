## E. The AP model — architecture stable

`arithmeticProgression.ts` adds a general arithmetic-sequence object: `a`,
`d`, an optional `length`, with `term`, `firstTerms`, `positionOf`,
`isTermOf`, `lastTerm`, `termFromEnd`, `sum`, `sumWithLast` and `directSum`
all derived. No new visual spec, no renderer, no `Chapter5APSpec` — this is a
mathematical model, not a representation extension, and is classified as
such.

## G. Common difference

`d` is stored **signed**, as a₂ − a₁. A test reverses a list and confirms the
sign flips, so 10, 7, 4 and 4, 7, 10 stay distinguishable. `d = 0` is a valid
AP and is taught. `isArithmetic` checks **every** consecutive pair — 2, 5, 8,
12 agrees on its first two differences and is correctly rejected.

## H. nth term — off-by-one

`term(ap, 1) === a` is asserted for every canonical AP, and `a + 1·d` is
asserted to differ wherever d is non-zero. Terms are also cross-checked
against repeated addition for eight positions on each AP. Non-integer and
non-positive positions throw rather than returning a plausible number.

## I. Membership

Positions are exact rationals. 74 gives n = 24 and is a term; 73 gives
**71/3** and is not; 100 gives 98/3 and is not. A test confirms the rounded
position names a *different* value (74, not 73), which is why rounding is
wrong rather than imprecise. A 13/2 position is rejected under any tolerance.
A value below the first term gives n = 0 and is rejected. The constant AP is
handled without dividing by zero.

## J. Sum derivation

Derived by reversing and adding **column by column**, with the cancellation
located between the two rows. The lessons say "two rows", not "pairing",
precisely because the pairing picture raises a false worry about odd n — and
a test runs odd n from 1 to 11 against direct addition.

The second form is obtained by substituting l = a + (n − 1)d, so the two can
never disagree; a test checks both forms at every n for every finite AP.

## K. Direct-sum reconciliation

Every authored sum is reconciled against direct addition: 185, −165, 348,
740, 110, 7500, 630, 3125, 87. `aₙ = Sₙ − Sₙ₋₁` is tested across four APs,
since the lessons rely on the term/sum relationship conceptually.

## L. Term versus cumulative total

The seats contrast runs the same AP twice: the 20th row holds **56** seats,
the first 20 rows hold **740**. Both numbers are authored and both are
tested. The point the lesson makes is that neither calculation fails or looks
odd — only the wording of the question distinguishes them.

The savings context carries negative d, and a test confirms every one of its
twelve terms stays positive, so the model does not outlive the situation.
