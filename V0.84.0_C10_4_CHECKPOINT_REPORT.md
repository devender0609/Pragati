## C/K. The real-root boundary

`auditRealRootScope` flags claims that outrun the scope — *no solution*,
*cannot be solved*, *has no roots*, *every quadratic has real roots* — while
passing *no real roots* and *in the real numbers*. Not a word ban: the audit
reads the sentence.

Seven fixtures, four that must fire and three that must not, all correct. A
companion audit forbids complex-number instruction outright.

**Two false positives of my own making, both fixed:**

1. It flagged *"An expression has no roots"* — which is **true**; an
   expression is not something a value can make true. The audit now requires
   the sentence to be about an equation.
2. It flagged the sentence *warning against* saying "no solution". A claim
   named in order to reject it is teaching the distinction, not making it.

Then a third finding, in the opposite direction: a lesson of mine **passed for
the wrong reason**. The prompt *"2x² + x + 4 = 0 has no solution"* escaped
because the audit looked for the word *equation* and the equation was written
symbolically. Fixed by recognising `= 0`, and the prompt reworded to attribute
the claim explicitly.

## F/G. Formula and discriminant

The derivation preserves every step the prompt names: a ≠ 0, division of
**every** term by a, (b/2a)², the ± at the square-root step, and the whole
numerator over 2a. A test checks each, and another scans for the formula
written without the ±.

**Thirteen discriminant claims recomputed**, each against its stated sign,
its claimed nature, and the actual number of real roots. Where D = 0 the
repeated root is confirmed to be −b/2a and to satisfy the equation. A lesson
claiming D > 0 while producing one root would fail.

"Two equal roots" is taught as *the same number arising twice*, not two
values.

## J. Contextual roots

The rectangle's −12 is shown to **genuinely solve the equation** before being
set aside, and the reason given is that lengths are positive — a fact about
rectangles. A test asserts the lessons say the sign alone is never the reason,
and forbids the rule "reject negative roots".

The wave also says plainly that **most quadratics do not factorise over the
integers**, asserted by test. Exercise sets suggest otherwise, and students
read a failure to factorise as their own mistake.
