## C. The 0°/90° source treatment

and 90 degrees by a limiting argument"*.

So no derivation from a figure, no unit circle. The lessons reason about

say plainly that the values at the ends are adopted rather than derived.

## E/F. Special-angle model — verdict B

`specialAngles.ts` is the one source of truth for all five angles and all six
ratios. Three things earn B rather than "two table rows":

**Undefined is a kind.** `TrigValue` is either `{kind:'defined'}` or
`{kind:'undefined', reason}`. Never Infinity, never NaN, never a sentinel.
`valueOf` **throws** on an undefined ratio rather than returning a number, and
a test confirms the stored object has no `exact` field and its JSON contains
no `Infinity` or `NaN`.

**Only sin and cos are authored.** tan, cot, sec and cosec are derived by
exact division, so a zero denominator *produces* undefined rather than being
annotated as such. The four gaps are a consequence of the arithmetic.

**Values are exact**, as (p/q)√k with perfect squares pulled out. A
source-conventional written form may be supplied — NCERT writes 1/√3 where
the canonical form is √3/3 — but `writtenMatches` **checks it numerically**,
so the display cannot drift from the mathematics.

`STANDARD_ANGLE_RATIOS` is now **derived from this module** rather than
holding its own copy. Its shape, its type and all nine values are unchanged;
a second table would have been a second thing to disagree with.

## H. The table

All five angles × six ratios validated against an explicit expected table,
plus three structural invariants: sin² + cos² = 1 at every angle; reciprocals
multiply to 1 wherever both are defined and are undefined wherever the base
is zero; tan = sin/cos wherever cos ≠ 0.

## I. Undefined values

Exactly four: **cosec 0°, cot 0°, tan 90°, sec 90°**, each carrying the
reason. The lesson sets `cot 90° = 0` beside `tan 90° undefined` — same
angle, one a value and one not — and explains that 1/0 asks which number
times 0 gives 1, which nothing does.

`auditInvalidDivision` catches "tan 90 is infinity", "1/0 is a very large
number", and dividing by cos²A at 90° or sin²A at 0°. Zero findings in the
authored lessons.

## J. The Pythagorean identity proof

Six steps, from GIVEN and TO PROVE: Pythagoras gives o² + a² = h²; divide by
h², **stating that h > 0 because it is a hypotenuse of a genuine triangle**;
recognise o²/h² as sin²A and a²/h² as cos²A; conclude.

The lesson says what is actually new — dividing by h² removes the scale, so a
relation about one triangle's lengths becomes a relation about the angle.

A PROVE unit that merely states the identity fails `auditProofScope`, tested.

**The acute proof is kept separate from the boundary check.** The lesson says
the triangle argument covers acute A, and that 0² + 1² = 1 at the ends is a
verification, not an extension of the proof.

## K. Derived identities

Both derived, neither listed. Dividing by cos²A gives 1 + tan²A = sec²A **where
cos A ≠ 0**; dividing by sin²A gives 1 + cot²A = cosec²A **where sin A ≠ 0**.
The condition is stated inside the derivation, and the lesson notes that each
excluded angle is exactly where the ratios involved are undefined anyway.

## L/M. Applications, and sanity versus proof

One-directional LHS→RHS chains, each step justified. The
cancelling-across-addition error gets its own treatment: (sin A + cos A)/sin A
is 1 + cot A, because sin A is a term and not a factor.

Six authored identities are evaluated at every standard angle where both
sides are defined. **This is regression evidence, not proof**, and the report,
the tests and the lesson all say so — the lesson adds the sharper point that
a single disagreeing angle *disproves* an identity, so checking is an
efficient error-catcher and nothing more. `auditProofByExample` catches
"checking at 30, 45 and 60 … so the identity is proved" and passes its
correction.
