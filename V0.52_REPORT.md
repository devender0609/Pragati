# Pragati v0.52 — Measurement Integrity and Growth Integration

Written 2026-08-18. Verified from a clean `npm ci`.

**Pragati is not a validated Growth assessment.** No percentile, no
RIT-like score, no mastery claim, and no calibrated item exists.

---

**Auditing the v0.51 report against source found one gap it did not
admit.** The report acknowledged the Growth components were unwired. It
did not mention that **`itemUse` was never enforced in the real practice
launchers**: `buildChapterSessionPlan` and `modulePracticeBlueprint`
filtered by `skillId` only. Separation existed as a module plus tests,
while the running code simply happened to receive a clean bank. The
first Growth item authored into `ITEMS` would have flowed into practice.
Both assemblers now call `itemsFor(..., 'practice')` before anything
else.

## 2. Eligibility gates (§2)

`src/features/assessment/growthEligibility.ts`

v0.51's `requireSpecification()` proved a secure item pointed at a
syntactically valid specification. Under it, an item could reach a field
test with a **draft, unreviewed** specification and no content, language,
or accessibility review — none was checked anywhere.

`evaluateGrowthItemEligibility()` is now the single authority. Field
test requires: valid specification, matching intended use, expert-review
status, explicit `fieldTestEligible`, a scorable format, mathematical
content + curriculum alignment + accessibility review, language review
where reading load is non-minimal, no instructional exposure, no
security flag, and a permitting lifecycle status.

Operational additionally requires: completed field test, calibration
version, active calibration, difficulty parameter, accepted fit,
completed DIF review with no unresolved flags, and operational approval.

Deny-by-default, and it returns **every** failed requirement rather than
the first.

---

## 3. Field test vs operational (§3)

The single `growth` context is now `growth_field_test` and
`growth_operational`. `contextSupported('growth_operational')` returns
`false` at product level — not merely unpopulated, but disabled, because
no item could pass the gate even if marked operational.

Field-test administration collects research evidence and produces no
achievement result. That is a design statement, not a limitation to be
removed later: the instrument is what is being studied.

---

## 4. AssessmentAssembler (§4)

v0.51 ended in `secure.slice(0, targetLength)` — the first N items in
array order, satisfying no blueprint.

`assembleAssessment()` filters for grade band, language, allowed format,
and exposure ceiling; then satisfies domain minimums, fills toward
target shares, and tops up within domain maxima, spreading difficulty
within each domain and honouring enemy-item constraints.

**It never relaxes a constraint.** An unsatisfiable blueprint returns a
failure naming the domain and the shortfall. A test asserts an assembled
form spans every blueprint domain — the property `slice()` could not
have.

---

## 5. Administration specification versioning (§5)

`PILOT_ADMINISTRATION_V1` replaces the `PILOT_TEST_LENGTH = 35`
constant. Length, bounds, duration, domain weights, formats, difficulty
targets, and exposure ceiling are versioned data with
`evidenceStatus: 'research_hypothesis'` and a rationale stating plainly
that none of the values is empirically supported. A test asserts a
different length works without code changes, and that
`evidenceStatus: 'validated'` is rejected.

## 6. Framework review against primary sources (§9)

`docs/MATHEMATICS_COMPETENCY_FRAMEWORK_REVIEW.md`

**A primary source was obtained and read in full**: the CBSE Curriculum
2026-27 for Class IX Mathematics, which reproduces the NCF-SE 2023
Curricular Goals CG-1…CG-11 verbatim with competency codes.

Findings that change the framework:

- **Computational Thinking (CG-9) is a first-class curricular goal** —
  decomposition, algorithms, generalising procedures — attached to
  almost every chapter in the CBSE syllabus. Pragati's draft framework
  **has no such domain at all.** This is the clearest gap.
- **Data and Probability are ONE goal (CG-6)**, not two. Splitting them
  at 10% and 5% of a 35-item form yields ~3 and ~2 items — unreportable
  even descriptively.
- **Patterns has no separate goal**; sequences map to algebra and
  modelling. Recommend merging into Algebra above the foundational
  stage.
- **Reasoning (CG-2, CG-7) and Modelling (CG-8) are always attached to
  content.** Recommend reclassifying both as cross-cutting processes.
- **Coordinate geometry and trigonometry sit inside CG-4 Geometry**, not
  algebra.

Proposed: six content domains plus three cross-cutting processes. On a
35-item form that averages ~6 items per domain instead of 3.5.

**Not applied in code.** Restructuring the framework needs human expert
sign-off — it should not be done by the same process that proposed it.
`frameworkSourceStatus` stays `draft_internal`.

**A structural finding that should reshape v0.53:** CBSE has published
the NCF-aligned 2026-27 curriculum for **Classes 9–12 only**. No
NCF-aligned middle-stage syllabus was found. Pragati's pilot targets
Classes 5–8 — the stage with the *weakest* primary evidence.

## 7. Rational Number progression (§10)

Preserved unchanged. The review distinguishes the two claims it makes:
node **ordering** is a mathematical-dependency claim (you cannot compare
unlike denominators before generating equivalents), while **grade
placement** is a curricular claim. The former is defensible from
mathematics; the latter needs primary evidence that, for Classes 6–8,
does not currently exist. Per-node classification against primary
sources remains outstanding.

## 8. Primary curriculum verification (§11)

**Unchanged and unpromoted.** Class 6 remains `secondary_corroborated`.
`ncert.nic.in` blocks automated access; per instruction I did not retry
it this iteration. The manual checklist recorded in v0.51
(`MANUAL_VERIFICATION_STEPS.class6`) stands. Grade 7 remains blocked by

Worth noting: `cbseacademic.nic.in` **is** fetchable, so the blocker is
specific to the NCERT textbook host, not to Indian government domains
generally.

## 9. Accommodations (§12)

v0.51 said an accommodated sitting "may not be comparable", full stop —
both over- and under-cautious. Now four categories (universal feature,
designated support, accommodation, modification) with per-support
records naming the barrier removed, possible construct impact, and
comparability status.

Magnification is a universal feature expected to be comparable. Extended
time and read-aloud require evidence — read-aloud's impact depends on
the item's language load, which is why specifications record it. A
calculator where the item prohibits one is a **modification**, flagged
potentially non-comparable. Administration comparability takes the most
cautious status used.

## 10. Multilingual architecture (§13)

`ItemFamily` with per-language variants. The load-bearing decision:
**each variant carries its own field-test and DIF status**. A form
field-tested in English is not field-tested in Hindi, and translating
the words does not make two forms equivalent — equivalence is an
empirical finding about student responses. Adjudication, equivalence
review, terminology review, and reading-load review are all required
before a variant is field-test eligible. No translated items created.

## 11. Growth UI integration (§14)

An **Assess** tab now exists in TeacherShell and renders
`GrowthAssignPanel`. Because no eligible bank exists, it states *"This
assessment cannot be assigned yet"*, explains that no items have been
authored from the existing specifications, and **disables the assign
action**. Students cannot launch a Growth session; navigation remains
four tabs with no fifth added.

## 13. Simplicity audit (§16)

Screenshot-based, partial. Two findings:

1. **Fixed** — the Assess panel overflowed 320px by 20px: the
   ineligibility detail cites file paths, which are unbreakable tokens.
2. **Found, not fixed** — the teacher class selector defaults to *"All
   local data (aggregate)"*. v0.50 §8 said aggregate should be an
   explicit secondary choice, not the default. It should default to the
   teacher's only classroom when exactly one exists.

A full screen-by-screen audit was not completed.

## 14. Response-quality metadata (§18)

Records response time, interruptions, omissions, resume events, device,
and connectivity per administration. `summariseResponseQuality()`
returns `verdict: 'no_automated_verdict'`. **No rapid-guessing threshold
is applied** — a 900ms response is recorded, not flagged. Invalidating a
child's results on an arbitrary cutoff is worse than keeping messy data.

## 17. Unresolved risks

**Scientific**

1. No primary competency source for Classes 6–8 — the pilot's own stage.
2. PARAKH has never been reviewed. It is the national assessment
   framework and the most relevant single source for this product.
3. NCF-SE 2023 itself unread; evidence is CBSE's faithful-looking
   reproduction.
4. Whether one Mathematics scale holds across Classes 5–8 is unknown;
   dimensionality could invalidate single-score reporting.
5. The rational-number grade placements are inferred, not sourced.

**Product**

6. Readiness not surfaced in Admin/Research.
7. Teacher context defaults to aggregate.
8. Bundle >500 kB; no content migrated.
9. Class 6 curriculum unverified from primary source.

## 18. READY or NOT READY for pilot item authoring?

### NOT READY.

Four of the six preconditions are met: eligibility gates, field-test /
operational separation, the blueprint assembler, and the review-evidence
model all exist and are enforced.

Two are not:

- **Framework primary-source review is incomplete.** One stage reviewed,
  and it produced substantive recommendations — add Computational
  Thinking, merge Data with Probability, reclassify Reasoning and
  Modelling — that have not been applied or expert-approved. Authoring
  items against domains that are about to change means re-tagging every
  item and re-running alignment review.
- **The rational-number progression has not been reviewed** per node
  against primary evidence, and the middle-stage evidence needed to do
  it has not been located.

Authoring now would produce items needing re-review before they could be
field-tested — which is the expensive step, not the authoring.

## 19. Recommended v0.53

1. **Obtain PARAKH and NCF-SE 2023.** Highest value. Both are
   government publications; `cbseacademic.nic.in` is reachable, so this
   may be a search problem rather than an access problem.
2. **Locate NCERT Learning Outcomes for Classes 6–8** to close the
   middle-stage gap.
3. **Put the framework review to two Indian mathematics educators.**
   Then apply or reject the restructure — including whether
   Computational Thinking becomes a domain or a process.
4. **Human-verify Class 6** from the NCERT PDF (still ~20 minutes).
5. **Then author 2–3 items per specification** and run them through the
   eligibility gate end to end.
6. Surface readiness in Admin/Research; fix the aggregate default.

Steps 1–3 are research and human review, not coding. That is the honest
shape of the next iteration.
