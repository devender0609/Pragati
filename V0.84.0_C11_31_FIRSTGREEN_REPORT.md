## J/K/N. The axioms

An assignment is a probability when no mass is negative and the masses total
exactly 1. Invalid assignments are **refused, not repaired**: a total of
`6/5` is reported as such rather than rescaled, and a negative mass is not
clamped. `probabilityOf` refuses to compute with an invalid assignment at
all.

Event probability is the **sum of its outcomes' masses**, so it follows from
which outcomes the event contains. Everything is exact Rational — `1/6`,
never `0.1666…`.

## L. Equally likely, derived

With n equally likely outcomes the axioms **force** each to 1/n, and an
event with k of them to k/n. `classicalAgreesWithAssignment` confirms the
counting rule equals the axiomatic answer for every canonical event.

When equal likelihood is **not** established, `equallyLikelyAssignment`
refuses — and the checkpoint-10 guard in `sampleSpace` still refuses too.
Favourable-over-total never becomes the definition.

## M. The rules

The complement rule is derived from the partition (`partitions: true`,
`totalsOne: true`), not quoted. The addition rule subtracts the overlap
because adding counts it twice, and `derivedMatchesDirect` confirms the
rule's answer equals the probability of the union event itself — **one
truth, reached two ways**. A test shows the naive sum gives `1` instead of
`5/6`. The mutually exclusive form is a special case where the overlap is
`0`, not a second rule.
