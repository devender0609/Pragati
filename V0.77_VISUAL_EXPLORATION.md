## 1. Why v0.76 failed, in my own words

You were right and my report was too kind to itself. v0.76's Home is a
purple rounded rectangle on a beige page above white content. I changed
the *ground* and the *typeface* and the *artwork* and then arranged them
in the same shape as before: a hero block, a list, a strip. Better
materials, same building.

The specific error: I treated "not card-heavy" as the goal and got a
page with nothing on it. Removing boxes is not composition. The three
concepts below each commit to an actual structural idea.

## 2. The three directions

### A — Mathematical Editorial

A serious publication about mathematics. Scale, hairlines and whitespace
do all the grouping; there is not one card on the page. The chapter
title is set at 96px, the chapter numeral runs as a 32rem ghost behind
the artwork, and the section numbers in the contents are display type
rather than captions. The drawing is cropped by the page edge so it
reads as a plate rather than an illustration slot.

**Materially different because** it removes the container as a concept
entirely and uses typographic scale as the only hierarchy device.

- Strong: calm, expensive, unmistakably about mathematics. Ages cleanly
  from Class 6 to Class 12 without redesign — the same layout holds a
  calculus chapter.
- Weak: a magazine cannot show progress. A student six chapters in sees
  nothing here that feels like six chapters. It is also the least
  *exciting* of the three, and you asked for engaging.

### B — Learning Journey

The page is one continuous line travelling through all ten chapters,
drawn the way a kolam is drawn — a single stroke threading a lattice of
stations. Book order, position and distance remaining are read off one
figure instead of being stated in three labels. The current chapter is a
large station with its artwork inside it; the parts of that chapter
branch off as stops.

**Materially different because** the layout *is* the navigation. There
is no chapter list; there is a route.

- Strong: exploration and progress become the same picture. It is the
  only one of the three that gets *better* as a student accumulates
  history, which matters for a product meant to run Classes 1–12.
- Weak: **it breaks at 390** — see the capture, the station labels
  collide. A serpentine needs width. And a path implies a fixed route
  through mathematics, which is a curriculum claim Pragati should be
  careful about making.

### C — Math Studio

The mathematics is not illustrated on this page, it is *on* it, at
working size, doing something. A bar divides into fifths and three fill
while a point travels to the same length on a number line below — two
representations of three-fifths, animating in step, 640px wide. Modular
panels then dock and **overlap** the ink canvas's lower edge, which is
where the depth comes from: real foreground and background planes, not
shadows on boxes. The chapter shelf carries each chapter in its own hue.

**Materially different because** the hero is a live mathematical object
rather than a picture of one, and the page has genuine z-depth.

- Strong: the only one that looks like software for *doing* mathematics.
  Most colour, most energy, most obviously modern, and the only one
  where a screenshot undersells the real thing.
- Weak: a live object per chapter is ten bespoke interactive builds, not
  ten SVGs. It is by a wide margin the most expensive direction, and it
  will look thinnest on whichever chapter nobody has built the
  interactive for yet.

## 3. What I recommend

**C, with A's typography and A's discipline about containers.**

Reasons, in order of weight:

1. **It is the only one that answers your actual complaint.** You said
   the product does not look like mathematics and does not invite
   exploration. C puts a working fraction at 640px as the first thing on
   the page. A draws mathematics beautifully; C *does* mathematics.
2. **The depth is structural.** The panels overlapping the canvas edge
   is the one thing in these three concepts that cannot be achieved by
   changing colours, radii or shadows — which is the test you set in
   §16.
3. **Colour without rainbow.** The chapter shelf carries ten hues at
   tile scale with one saturated and nine tinted. That reads as a
   collection, not a database list, and not a rainbow.
4. **B is the better idea and the worse product.** The journey is more
   original and I would like to be recommending it. It fails at 390,
   and Pragati's students are overwhelmingly on phones. I would rather
   take B's *station* treatment into C's shelf later than ship a
   composition that needs a laptop.

What I would take from A into C: the 96px title, the ghost numeral, and
the rule that the contents list gets hairlines rather than a white
panel. C's "Open now" panel is still a white card, and it is the
weakest element on the page.

## 4. What still looks weak — before you decide

Said plainly, because you will see these anyway:

- **C's three docked panels are unequal in content and equal in height**,
  so the teal and ink panels have dead space at the bottom.
- **C's canvas has an empty region** to the lower right of the number
  line at 1440.
- **C's "Open now" is a white card** — the exact thing you rejected,
  surviving in the strongest concept.
- **B's copy is now slightly wrong**: "the line above fills in" describes
  behaviour I removed (see §5).
- **A's middle third is still beige**, which is one of your listed
  rejection conditions. Making the chapter shelf ink helped; the
  contents section did not change.
- **None of the three has been tested with a student who has real
  history.** All six captures are the first-run state, because that is
  the only honest state for a fresh profile. The concept that most
  depends on history (B) is therefore the one shown at its weakest.

## 5. Product semantics preserved (§17)

Every concept calls the same two functions Home calls —
`class6ChapterCards()` and `officialChapterRows()`. Availability, the
official/legacy provenance distinction, the publication gate and the
"part is genuinely open" rule are all inherited rather than restated.
No concept invents a chapter, a section, a percentage or a streak.
Unavailable chapters are non-interactive in all three. The §7.4
fingerprint, Practice eligibility and the Growth lock were not touched
because no product file was touched.

**One truth bug I caught and fixed mid-flight.** Direction B's first
version drew a saffron "distance travelled" segment from the start of
the book to the open chapter. It looked good and it was a lie — this
student has finished nothing, and a filled line through six chapters
claims they finished six. The line is now uniform, and the only emphasis
is the ring around the chapter that is genuinely open. That is exactly
the class of error §17 exists to prevent, and it appeared within twenty
minutes of starting to make the page prettier.
