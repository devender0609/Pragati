# A. Product

## 1. What materially changed visually?

Six things, in order of how much they change the impression:

1. **The brand.** A blue square containing the letter "P" — the placeholder
   every internal tool ships with — is replaced by a real mark: three ascending
   bars with a forward chevron. Pragati (प्रगति) means *progress*, and the mark
   draws exactly that; it also reads as a chart, which is honest for a product
   about a student improving over time. The Devanagari appears under the
   wordmark at full size, because this is an Indian product named with a Hindi
   word.

2. **Chapter identity.** Ten near-identical white rectangles became ten cards,
   each with its own accent and a motif drawn from *its own mathematics* — a
   compass for Constructions, a shaded quarter-circle for Fractions, two rays
   with the angle arc for Lines and Angles.

3. **Chapters as a pathway.** The Fractions page is now a numbered rail, because
   the chapter genuinely must be worked in sequence and a flat list of boxes
   does not say so.

4. **Lessons stopped being documents.** Six headings with unbroken paragraph
   runs became distinct, labelled surfaces.

5. **Coming-soon states.** Grey dashed boxes read as *broken*. They now keep
   their motif at low opacity with a quiet "Coming soon" pill — deliberate
   rather than half-built, and still plainly not tappable.

6. **A 44px floor on every control**, which fixed real defects (below).

## 2. What design system now exists?

A semantic palette in `tailwind.config.js` where **every colour has a job**:

| Token | Hue | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `brand` | indigo | identity, primary actions, focus |
| `learn` | violet | instruction — reading, explanation, worked examples |
| `practice` | teal | doing — guided and independent practice |
| `correct` | emerald | a confirmed correct answer |
| `attend` | amber | "needs another look" |
| `progress` | sky | factual activity counts |
| `teacher` | slate-blue | teacher-only material |

Plus ten chapter accents, assigned **by chapter number** so a chapter keeps its
colour everywhere, and used for recognition only — availability is never
signalled by hue.

**Two decisions worth defending.**

*Wrong answers are amber, never red.* A wrong answer in practice is not a
failure; it is the moment the teaching happens. Colouring it the colour of a
system error teaches a student to fear being wrong. Red stays reserved for
genuine destructive errors.

*Colour is never the only cue.* Every semantic surface pairs its hue with an
icon **and** a text label. A student who cannot distinguish violet from teal
still reads "Worked example" and "Try it yourself".

The 700 step of each ramp clears 4.5:1 on white; 500 is for fills and borders,
not text. `prefers-reduced-motion` disables every animation globally, and the
app works identically without them — motion is polish, never information.

## 3–4. How do student Home and Learn differ?

Class 6 Learn opens with a gradient header stating what the class *is*, then the
single clearest next action, then all ten chapters.

**The next-action card says "Start here", not "Continue learning".** Pragati
records no per-student section progress, so a "continue where you left off" card
would be fabricated progress — precisely what §34 and §36 forbid. A test asserts
the string "Continue learning" does not appear. On the in-shell chapter page,
where real recorded progress *does* exist, the same card correctly says
"Continue" when a section has been started and "Start next" otherwise.

**A late finding worth recording:** the first pass redesigned
`FractionsChapterLanding`, and visual QA revealed students actually reach a
*different* component — `OfficialChapterLanding`, rendered inside the student
shell. The redesign had missed the screen students really see. Both are now
redesigned. Screenshots caught this; no test would have.

## 5. How do lessons differ?

`src/design/LessonBlocks.tsx` provides `ConceptIntro`, `PriorKnowledge`,
`IdeaCard`, `MathVisualCard`, `WorkedExampleCard`, `NoticeThis`,
`CommonMistake`, `PracticeCard`, `ReasoningChallenge`, `SummaryCard` and
`TeacherNoteCard`.

**Presentation only.** No component alters, reorders, filters or reinterprets
any mathematical content — that constraint is what makes this safe to apply to
content hand-audited twice and educator-reviewed never.

Two specifics:

- **`MathText`** renders `3/8` in authored prose as a stacked fraction, and
  gives screen readers "3 over 8" rather than "3 slash 8". It is deliberately
  timid: only `digits/digits` is touched, so dates and word-ratios survive.
  Silently reformatting authored mathematics is how a presentation layer starts
  changing meaning.
- **The worked-example answer is a distinct terminal band.** Previously it was
  the last paragraph in the stack, so a student scanning back for it had to
  re-read the whole example.

Progressive disclosure was already the behaviour and is retained, now with a
"Next step · 2 of 4" counter so a student knows how much remains.

## 6. What did screenshots reveal?

Visual QA **measured the rendered DOM** rather than eyeballing images —
`documentElement.scrollWidth`, computed control heights, console and page
errors — at 390 / 768 / 1440. 31 screenshots in `screenshots-v069/`.

**Three real defects found, all fixed:**

1. **Tap targets below 44px across screens shipped since v0.55** — a `<select>`
   at 35px, inputs at 36px, buttons at 40px, one link-button at 28px. Fixed with
   a base-layer floor so it also covers controls nobody thought to audit, and
   controls added later.
2. **The wrong Fractions page had been redesigned** (§4 above).
3. Native radios and checkboxes are 16px *by design* — their tap target is the
   wrapping `<label>`. The QA script now measures the label, which is the honest
   check rather than demanding a 44px radio dot.

Final state: **no horizontal overflow, no console errors, no undersized tap
targets** at any width.

## 7. What remains visually weak?

- **The first-run home screen is untouched.** A student with no record still
  lands on the older three-card screen. It is legacy and out of v0.69's scope,
  but it is the first thing a new user sees.
- **Teacher and Admin got the palette and the 44px floor, not a redesign.** §14's
  separation is partial: the manual-verification tooling is correctly Admin-only,
  but teacher screens still carry more governance density than a teacher needs.
- **Age-stage direction is tokens, not implementation.** The design tokens can
  support the four bands; only Classes 6–8 are actually styled, as §13 intends.
- **Progress remains counts, not a visual path.** §36's "progress path" is not
  built, because Pragati records too little real activity to draw one honestly.

# B. Curriculum

## 8–9. How many grades are primary-verified?

**Five of twelve: Classes 6, 9, 10, 11, 12.** Unchanged in count from v0.68 —
and deliberately so, because verifying the other seven requires a person with a
textbook, not more code.

**Pending: Classes 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 7, 8.** `ncert.nic.in` disallows automated
access; no attempt was made to circumvent it. Their chapter counts remain
`null` — not zero, not inherited from the pre-NCF books.

## 10. What manual verification workflow exists?

Three pieces, replacing v0.68's prose instruction to *edit the TypeScript* —
which made curriculum verification a developer task when reading a contents page
is a five-minute job for a teacher.

1. **`manualCurriculumImport.ts`** — a JSON submission plus a validator that
   rejects: duplicate chapter numbers, **gaps in the numbering** (the likeliest
   real mistake, since it usually means a page was turned twice), blank titles
   (never inferred), a missing edition line, a non-date, and a verifier field
   that is not a person. `"verifier": "admin"` is refused — verification is
   attributable or it is not verification. Re-importing over an already-verified
   grade is refused too.
2. **An Admin screen** (`CurriculumVerificationPanel`) with a template button
   and inline validation.
3. **`MANUAL_CURRICULUM_VERIFICATION_CLASSES_1_5_7_8.md`** — which book to open
   per class, what to confirm on the imprint page, what to copy, how to enter it,
   and how to check the result.

**The panel validates but does not save.** A curriculum verified by a paste into
a browser and stored in localStorage is not auditable, and would be the weakest
link in a system built entirely on auditable evidence. It prints the exact record
for a human to commit, so a second person can check it against the book.

A test asserts the blank template is **not** importable — an empty form must
never count as a verification.

## 11. Are units, chapters and topics represented separately?

**Yes — and this was a real defect, not a refactor.**

v0.68 collapsed all three. Each curriculum now declares its `topLevel`, and each
unit records whether the source establishes chapters within it:

| Class | Source | Units | Chapters | Topics |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 6 | NCERT textbook | — | **10** | 65 |
| 9 | CBSE syllabus | **6** | **15** | 15 |
| 10 | CBSE syllabus | **7** | **UNKNOWN** | 15 |
| 11 | CBSE syllabus | **5** | **UNKNOWN** | 14 |
| 12 | CBSE syllabus | **6** | **UNKNOWN** | 13 |

Class IX is the interesting case: uniquely among IX–XII its syllabus prints an
explicit **Chapter Name** column, so its 15 chapters are real and the student
view now shows them instead of the six unit headings.

Classes X–XII return **null** for chapters. Several of their topic titles closely
resemble NCERT chapter names — *resembling is not evidence*, and §19 forbids
asserting the equivalence without the textbook.

## 12. Did any grade previously mislabel units as chapters?

**Yes. Class 10's student view said "7 chapters".** The 7 are CBSE syllabus
units: Unit II is "Algebra", which is not a chapter and contains four topics.
The UI was telling a student something the source does not say.

It now reads **"7 units"**, taken from `structureNoun()`, and a test asserts the
summary line contains "units" and **not** "chapters" — the defect written down as
an executable claim. Classes 11 and 12 had the same defect and the same fix. The
teacher table now has three separate columns, with a dash where a level does not
apply and "Unknown" where nobody has read it.

The v0.68 invariant holds: official curriculum and Pragati coverage stay
independent, Class 10 still shows all 7 official units with 0 lessons, and
`checkRegistryInvariants()` returns empty.

# C. Fractions

## 13–14. What content defects were fixed, and what mappings changed?

**A diagnosis was withdrawn** — the most important finding in this section.

v0.68 attached `mixed_number_is_multiplication` to a student entering **6/4** for
2¾, arguing that only 2 × 3 with the denominator carried unchanged reaches it.
Re-checked against §24's test — *could a student type this for another plausible
reason?* — **the argument fails: 2 + 4 = 6 also reaches 6/4**, by adding the
whole number to the denominator. That is a different error with different
teaching, and a student who made it would have been told they had multiplied,
which they had not.

Alternative numbers were tried and every one collided too: for 2⅔ the
multiplication route (4/3) collides with whole-plus-numerator; for 3¼ the route
(3/4) collides with reading the whole as a numerator. So the response is now
neutral, with a stronger hint, and `mixed_number_is_multiplication` stays
documented but undiagnosed.

**Diagnostic distractors: 9 → 8.** That is the number going *down* being the
right outcome. A wrong diagnosis is worse than none.

**§25 — numeric semantics verified** across equivalent, simplified and mixed
forms. For 1/2 + 1/3: `5/6` and `10/12` are both correct (equivalence accepted);
`2/5` is diagnosed; **`4/10` is not** — it equals 2/5 but is not evidence of the
same method. For 2¾: `11/4` correct, `22/8` incorrect (this item does not accept
equivalents, because it is about converting wholes into fourths).

**§28 — one procedure-without-a-reason found**, in §7.6. Six paragraphs build the
cutting argument, and then "multiply the top and bottom by the same number"
arrives asserted, with the link left for the student to supply. One sentence now
connects them: *"Cutting every part into 3 makes 3 times as many parts in the
whole, and 3 times as many of the parts you took. Both numbers grow by 3 because
the same cut did both."*

**§30 — contexts diversified.** Cake appeared five times and pizza once. Changed:
pizza → roti; one cake → one litre of milk shared among 8 glasses; another cake →
watermelon (kept as food, because unequal cutting is the point); chocolate bar →
laddoo. The same-whole argument keeps a food context because it genuinely needs
two objects of different size.

## 15. What visuals improved?

**§26 — a defect I introduced in v0.68 was removed.** The area-model options
carried an "unequal parts" text badge, added for legibility. It appeared **only
on the wrong options**, letting a student eliminate the diagnostic distractor
without reading the diagram — destroying the very evidence the format was added
to collect. Removed.

Also: option height 32px → **56px** (at 32px, telling a 1/2 part from a 1/6 part
was a squint, and a diagram that cannot be read is worse than none because it
looks like it worked); **arrow-key navigation added**, since `role="radio"` in a
`radiogroup` promises it and browsers do not provide it for buttons; and every
option now uses identical border weight, fill and background, so the only visual
difference between them is the geometry.

The `aria-label` still describes the partition exactly as a sighted student sees
it. Withholding it would not make the item fairer — it would make the diagram
sighted-only, and the diagram *is* the item.

## 16. What teacher notes improved?

**None were rewritten, and that is the finding.** All nine were audited against
§29's five questions. They already answer them — §7.8's note on using 1/4 + 1/4
as the diagnostic ("the wrong method gives 2/8, which equals one of the addends —
students can see for themselves it cannot be right") is exactly the kind of thing
§29 asks for. Rewriting them to show activity would have made them worse.

They remain unreviewed by any educator, which is the real gap.

## 17. Is §7.4's fingerprint unchanged?

**Yes. `a1a3ff57`, `S74-v1-A1A3FF`, artifact v1.** Untouched through a full
visual redesign, and asserted in the v0.69 suite as well as the v0.66–v0.68 ones.

## 18. Did real educator review arrive?

**No.** `SECTION_7_4_CLASSIFIED_FEEDBACK` is empty and a test asserts it. No
section is educator-reviewed; nothing is published.

# What must not be claimed

- **No section is educator-reviewed. Nothing is publishable.** Three iterations
  of hand audit have found eleven defects between them, which is the honest
  reason to expect a reviewer to find more.
- **8 diagnostic distractors means 8 *claims*** that a response admits one
  reading. Each is an argument a reviewer may reject — v0.67 had two rejected and
  v0.69 rejected a third.
- **Seven grades have an unknown chapter count.** The workflow to fix that exists
  and has not been run.
- **The visual work changed no curriculum claim, no eligibility rule, no review
  gate and no fingerprint.** Asserted, not assumed.
- **Growth is untouched.** No changes to CAT, scoring, norms, secure items,
  calibration or mastery claims.
