## 7. Computational Thinking decision

**Item-level process tag. NOT a Mathematics reporting domain.**

Evidence, all from the primary CBSE CT&AI document:

- §2: CT is integrated "across school subjects, including beyond
  Mathematics, **as a cross-cutting theme**".
- §3.1: learning standards are "foundational capacities that cut across
  disciplines".
- §9.2: assessed via projects, reflective journals, teacher observation.
- §10.2.2: the CT resource book parallels the Mathematics textbook —
  CT is delivered *through* Mathematics content, not beside it.

Reporting a CT score inside a Mathematics test would attribute a
cross-subject capability to one subject, and a 35-item selected-response
form cannot measure what CBSE assesses through projects. Tagging
preserves the information for later study without the claim.

**Flagged for expert review** — CT is a major official emphasis and
schools may reasonably expect to see it.

---

## 8. Rational Number domain decision

**Keep as a separate reported domain — labelled explicitly as a
`PRAGATI MEASUREMENT-DESIGN DISTINCTION, NOT AN OFFICIAL CURRICULUM
DISTINCTION`.**

NCF-SE places rational numbers inside CG-1 with no separate goal. The
separation is justified by developmental progression and instructional
usefulness, not by curriculum. The code says so in those words, and a
test asserts the wording, so it cannot quietly drift into an alignment
claim.

## 18. Verdict

# NOT READY FOR ITEM AUTHORING

The technical gates are in place: eligibility gating, field-test /
operational separation, blueprint assembly, evidence and review models,
node-level evidence classification, and the crosswalk.

It is not the software that blocks authoring. It is that:

1. **No human expert has reviewed the framework.** The package is
   ready; nobody has completed it.
2. **Five of twelve nodes have uncertain official placement**, and the
   middle-stage source needed to fix that has not been located.
3. **PARAKH — the national assessment framework — has not been read.**
   Writing items for an Indian assessment without it risks a structural
   mismatch discovered late.

Authoring now would produce items needing re-tagging and re-review.

*(The prescribed verdict "TECHNICALLY READY — AWAITING HUMAN EXPERT
APPROVAL" was considered and rejected: it implies only sign-off is
missing, whereas evidence gaps 2 and 3 are substantive and could still
change the framework.)*
