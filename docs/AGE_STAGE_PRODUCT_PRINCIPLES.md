# Age-Stage Product Principles

Deferred from v0.52 and v0.53. These principles guide future work; they
are **not** a mandate to redesign existing screens.

Three separate concerns, deliberately not merged:

- **Visual design** — how it looks.
- **Cognitive load** — how much a child is asked to hold at once.
- **Assessment administration** — how a formal session is delivered.

The first is often mistaken for the whole problem. It is the least
important of the three.

---

## Classes 1–2 — Foundational

**Visual:** one action per screen; artwork carries meaning rather than
decorating; touch targets ≥60px (larger than the 44px baseline);
minimal text, and no text that must be read to answer.

**Cognitive load:** one instruction at a time; no multi-step navigation;
no progress percentages — a child this age does not benefit from them;
concrete representations before symbols.

**Administration:** audio-delivered; adult present; 10–15 minutes
maximum; disengagement treated as a data-quality signal rather than a
wrong answer.

---

## Classes 3–5 — Preparatory

**Visual:** strong chapter identity through colour and illustration;
visual worked examples; simple progress (a bar, not a percentage).

**Cognitive load:** short sentences; one new idea per screen;
manipulatives and models before abstract notation.

**Administration:** 15–25 minutes; reading load minimised; a practice
item before the real set.

---

## Classes 6–8 — Middle

**Visual:** concept pathways showing how ideas connect; diagrams that
carry mathematical meaning; misconception feedback that names the
misconception.

**Cognitive load:** multi-step problems acceptable; reasoning prompts
appropriate; progress by concept rather than a single number.

**Administration:** ~35 items in ~40 minutes (a hypothesis, not a
finding); no hints; clear session boundaries.

---

## Classes 9–12 — Secondary

**Visual:** mature, dense but clean; formulas and graphs first-class;
efficient navigation over hand-holding.

**Cognitive load:** highest tolerance; multi-part items acceptable;
revision and readiness framing is appropriate here and nowhere earlier.

**Administration:** longest sessions tolerable; exam-preparation framing
legitimate.

---

## What these principles forbid

- Reusing the Class 12 information density for Class 3 with a bigger
  font. Stage adaptation is about *what is shown*, not size.
- Exam-readiness framing below Class 9.
- Percentages and scores for foundational learners.
- Assuming one test length fits Classes 5 to 8.
