## 2. Middle Stage Mathematics Curricular Goals — as printed

There are **ten** curricular goals, CG-1 to CG-10.

### CG-1 — Understands numbers and sets of numbers (whole numbers, fractions, integers, rational numbers, real numbers), looks for patterns, and appreciates relationships between numbers

| ID | Competency (as printed) |
| :--- | :--- |
| C-1.1 | Develops a sense for and an ability to manipulate (read, write, form, compare, estimate, apply operations to) and name in words large whole numbers up to 20 digits, and express them in scientific notation using exponents and powers |
| C-1.2 | Discovers, identifies, and explores patterns in numbers and describes rules for their formation (e.g. multiples of 7, powers of 3, prime numbers), and explains relations between different patterns |
| C-1.3 | Learns about the inclusion of zero and negative quantities as numbers, and the arithmetic operations on them, as given by Brahmagupta |
| C-1.4 | Explores and understands sets of numbers — whole numbers, fractions, integers, rational numbers, real numbers — and their properties, and **visualises them on the number line** |
| C-1.5 | Explores the idea of percentage and applies it to solve problems |
| C-1.6 | Explores and applies fractions (both as ratios and in decimal form) in daily-life situations |

### CG-2 — Understands the concepts of variable, constant, coefficient, expression, and (one-variable) equation, and uses these to solve meaningful daily-life problems with procedural fluency

| ID | Competency |

## 3. Interpretation for Classes 6–8

**There is no per-grade split in NCF-SE.** The goals are stated for the
stage as a whole. Ganita Prakash Grade 6 realises a subset; Grades 7 and
8 continue the same goals. This means:

- A Class 6 unit maps to a **stage-level** competency, not a
  "Class 6 competency". There is no such thing in the source.
- Absence of a topic from Grade 6 does **not** place it outside the
  Middle Stage. C-1.6 (fractions as ratios and in decimal form) and
  C-2.1–C-2.5 (algebra) are Middle Stage competencies regardless of
  which grade's textbook carries them. **This directly bears on the
  legacy-module question** — see
  `CLASS6_LEGACY_MODULE_DISPOSITION_REVIEW.md`.

## 4. Implications for Pragati

### 4.1 Number line is an explicit competency, not an incidental tool

C-1.4 names visualising number sets **on the number line** as a
competency in its own right. Ganita Prakash §7.4 ("Marking Fraction
Lengths on the Number Line") is its direct Grade 6 realisation, and
Pragati has no coverage of it. This raises the priority of §7.4 from
"one missing section" to "an uncovered named competency."

### 4.2 Compass-and-straightedge construction is an explicit competency

C-3.4 names it. Ganita Prakash Chapter 8 ("Playing with Constructions")
is its realisation, and Pragati has nothing at all for it. This cannot
be covered by multiple-choice items.

### 4.3 Computational thinking (CG-8) is a first-class goal

Confirmed at Middle Stage, as v0.52 concluded at Secondary Stage. Ganita
Prakash Chapter 3 ("Number Play") is its principal vehicle. Pragati has
no equivalent anywhere.

### 4.4 History of mathematics (CG-9) is a first-class goal

Two competencies, naming specific mathematicians. Pragati treats history
as decoration where it appears at all. Ganita Prakash §7.9 ("A Pinch of
History") and §10.5 are direct realisations.

### 4.5 Data and statistics are ONE goal (CG-5), and probability is absent

At Middle Stage there is **no probability competency at all**. The v0.52
finding that "Data and Probability are one goal (CG-6)" was a Secondary
Stage reading. At Middle Stage it is CG-**5**, and it is data only.

### 4.6 Reasoning (CG-6) and puzzles (CG-7) are separate standalone goals

Both are content-independent process goals at Middle Stage. v0.52
recommended treating reasoning as a cross-cutting process rather than
content-attached; at Middle Stage the framework already does this. That
recommendation should be re-examined against this evidence rather than
carried forward.
