## D/E. Architecture — general extension

Checkpoint 60's `LinearSystemGraphSpec` draws a straight line from two
endpoints, which cannot describe a curve, and nothing else in the codebase
samples a function. So `PolynomialGraphSpec` holds the **polynomial** and
`samplePoints` evaluates it — general for polynomials of any degree, with
nothing in it that knows about Chapter 2. No `PolynomialGraphSpec` variant
per chapter, and no general-purpose graphing engine.

`s2_2_zeroes_meaning_lesson`, `s2_2_counting_zeroes_lesson`,

## H/I. Zero validation and graph reconciliation

Every claimed zero is checked to make p(x) **exactly** 0, not small. Every
sampled point is asserted to satisfy y = p(x), and the drawn x-intercepts are
asserted equal to the algebraic zeroes.

Two negative fixtures: a window clipped so a zero falls outside is rejected
(*"falls outside the drawn window"*), and alt text claiming two intercepts for
the repeated-zero curve is rejected.

## J/K. Factorisation and coefficient relations

Four factorisations expand back to their exact coefficients, including the
non-monic 2(x − 3)(x + 1/2) and the scaled 3(x − 2)(x − 3).

Sum and product are recomputed for five polynomials and five sign fixtures
covering positive and negative b and c. A test asserts **b/a differs from the
sum** wherever b is non-zero, so the classic dropped-minus error cannot pass.
The relation is also cross-checked against the zeroes added and multiplied
directly.

The minus sign is **derived**, not announced: it comes from the brackets
subtracting the zeroes in (x − α)(x − β).

## L. Repeated and no-real-zero cases

`rationalZeroes` returns a repeated zero **once** — one x-intercept, one
number — while the relations still use it twice. The lesson says the zero
occurs twice *in the factorisation* and is a single point on the axis.

"Meets the x-axis" is used throughout rather than "crosses", with the reason
stated, and a test requires both the wording and its justification.

No-real-zero wording reuses the checkpoint-58 discipline: "no real zeroes",
never "no zeroes".

## M. Scale freedom

`3x² − 15x + 18` carries the same zeroes as `x² − 5x + 6` with every
coefficient different and a y-intercept of 18 against 6. The construction
lesson gives the general answer as k(x² − Sx + P) and names k = 1 as a
**choice**, with a practice item on what one extra fact would pin k down.

The y-intercept is tested never to coincide with a zero, and the lessons say
in words that it is not one.
