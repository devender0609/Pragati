# Chapter 3 — Number Play: what to send, and what to ask

**Status: NOT SENT.** Nothing in this chapter has been reviewed,
approved or published, and nothing will be until an educator responds.

## What to send

The folder **`PRAGATI_CHAPTER_3_REVIEW_PACKAGES/`** contains three reviewer
files plus a manifest (`index.json`). **Send the reviewer the three markdown
files only.** `index.json` is Pragati's record of the codes, fingerprints and
artifact versions; the reviewer does not need it, and each markdown file
already carries its own review code.

| File | Section | Artifact |
|---|---|---|
| `SECTION_3_1_FOR_REVIEWER.md` | 3.1 Numbers can Tell us Things | v2 |
| `SECTION_3_2_FOR_REVIEWER.md` | 3.2 Supercells | v1 |
| `SECTION_3_3_FOR_REVIEWER.md` | 3.3 Patterns of Numbers on the Number Line | v2 |

§3.1 and §3.3 are at **artifact version 2** because they were rewritten,
not edited, after the primary pages were read. If the reviewer saw an
earlier draft of either, it was a different lesson and should be
discarded.

## Who it goes to

A **Grade 6 mathematics teacher** who has taught from Ganita Prakash, or
is willing to read pp. 55–60 alongside the packages.

## What they are being asked

Whether each lesson teaches the mathematics of its section well enough
to use — as a teacher, not as a proofreader. Each package lists the
specific places where Pragati chose something the book does not state,
and those are the parts most worth their judgement:

- **§3.1** — plants of stated heights instead of the book's children;
  and stating outright that the tallest always reports 0, where the
  source leads students to it through questions.
- **§3.2** — the tie case. Two equal neighbours mean neither is a
  supercell. The source's tables use distinct numbers and never raise
  it. We think it sharpens the definition; a teacher may think it
  distracts from the section's own emphasis.
- **§3.3** — the number-line windows, including an 86,000–88,000 line
  whose interval is 250 rather than a round thousand; and naming the
  divide-the-span method explicitly.

## How responses come back

Each package carries a **review code** (for example `S31-v1-20BA61`) and
sixteen questions.

**The `v1` in a review code is the version of the review question set (the
instrument), not the lesson.** All three Chapter 3 codes read `v1` because
all three use version 1 of the questions — including §3.1 and §3.3, whose
lessons are at artifact version 2. The lesson being reviewed is identified by
its artifact version and content fingerprint, as recorded in `index.json`:

| Section | Review code | Artifact version | Fingerprint |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3.1 | `S31-v1-20BA61` | 2 | `20ba61e4` |
| 3.2 | `S32-v1-4F4A92` | 1 | `4f4a922f` |
| 3.3 | `S33-v1-F85783` | 2 | `f85783be` | For each: **accept**, **revise**, **reject** or
**insufficient evidence**, with a reason. A decision without a reason
cannot be adjudicated and is treated as unanswered.

The code's fingerprint part fixes which artifact was reviewed. If a lesson changes after
the package is sent, the fingerprint changes and the response is
matched to the version it was actually about.

## What is not being asked

Anything about Fractions. That chapter has its own packages and its own
covering note — `REVIEW_HANDOFF/SEND_THIS.md` — and its own separate
obligation about student-facing misconception wording that does not
apply here.
