## D/E. Architecture — verdict **B**

`rightTriangleRoles`, exact-angle validation and `parallelGroups` all carried
over and are reused. But the audit found the gap the risk rating predicted:
**nothing could express observer eye height, the horizontal reference through
the eye, or how the triangle's vertical leg relates to the object's height.**
The words "elevation", "depression" and "observer" appear nowhere in the
geometry module.

Those are exactly the listed failure modes, and they are invisible in a
figure — a correct tan 60 applied to the wrong vertical gives a tidy number
and a false answer.

So `observationModel.ts` holds the observation as one object and derives the
rise, the total height, the line of sight, the triangle's coordinates and the
figure from it. It is general for elevation and depression observations;
nothing in it knows about Chapter 9. No `HeightsAndDistancesFigureSpec`.

Two choices worth naming:

- **`targetQuantity` is explicit** — `total_height`, `rise_above_eye` or
  `horizontal_distance`. This is what stops "always add the eye height"
  being encodable as a habit: whether it enters is derived, not assumed.
- **The triangle is derived from `direction`**, so an elevation whose sight
  line falls, or a depression angle placed at the lower object, is not
  expressible rather than merely discouraged.

## G/H. Elevation, depression and observer height

The horizontal runs through the **eye**, and a test confirms the reference
line's two endpoints share the eye's height exactly. `recomputedAngle` derives
the angle from the coordinates and is asserted equal to the claim for every
observation — the checkpoint-55 check, applied to a new kind of figure.

Three cases are authored deliberately:

- **ground level** (eye height 0): the leg *is* the full height, nothing added
- **eye at 1.5 m, asked for the tower**: 24 m rise + 1.5 = **25.5 m**
- **the same observation, asked for the rise above eye level**: **24 m**, and
  adding would answer a different question

The checkpoint's named fixture is implemented with original values: a 28 m
rise with a 1.6 m eye gives **29.6 m**, and a test asserts the answer is not
the 28 m rise.

## I. Line of sight

Tested longer than both legs on every observation, and `validateObservation`
rejects a degenerate triangle where it is not. A guided example gives the line
of sight instead of the horizontal distance, so **sine** is the right ratio —
tangent is taught as common, not mandatory, and a test refuses any unrefuted
"always use tangent".

## J. Chapter 8 agreement

Every ratio is read from `STANDARD_ANGLE_RATIOS` by identity — a test asserts
`tanOf(d) === row.tan`, not merely equal values. A Chapter 9 lesson cannot
disagree with Chapter 8 because it has no separate table to disagree from.

## K. Parallel-horizontal reasoning

The elevation-depression equality is **derived**: the observer's horizontal
and the ground are parallel, the line of sight is a transversal, and the two
are alternate angles. A test requires "alternate angles", "transversal" and
"parallel" in the prose and forbids "depression always equals". One practice
item asks what happens over sloping ground, so the derivation is load-bearing.

## L/M. Numerical and unit fidelity

Recomputed: 10√3 ≈ 17.32, 20√3 ≈ 34.64, 24, 25.5, 29.6, 15√3 ≈ 25.98, and the
two-triangle result AF = 60 m with a 40 m walk. Exact forms are rationalised
(30/√3 → 10√3). A test forbids writing `=` between a surd and its decimal.

**One defect fixed here:** the far distance computed as `h / tan 30` gave
59.999999999999986, because √3 × √3 is not exactly 3 in binary, and the
figure's coordinates would have carried it. It is now rounded at the model
boundary, with the reason recorded in the code.

## N. Defects found and fixed

2. **The two-triangle figure asserted a right-triangle role at B for a leg it

3. `validateObservation` **threw** on an out-of-scope angle instead of
