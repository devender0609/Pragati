## 1. Why Class 6 read "Units 10 / Chapters 10"

`officialUnitCount()` returned `c.units.length` unconditionally — and
`units` is the name of the **storage array**, not a claim about the
book. For Classes 9-12 that array does hold units. For Class 6 it holds
top-level **chapter** records, so the function reported 10 units for a
textbook that defines none, and the coverage matrix printed a layer of
hierarchy Ganita Prakash does not have.

The source inventory was right the whole time; the coverage matrix read
an implementation detail as terminology. A storage-array name must not
define curriculum vocabulary.

## 2-3. The final hierarchy

| Class | Units | Chapters | Sections | Topics |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 6 | — | 10 | 65 | — |
| 9 | 6 | 15 | — | 15 |
| 10–12 | verified | — | — | verified |

Four columns, because the sources do not share one shape. A dash means
the source does not define that level, not that we failed to count it.

**Sections and topics are now different levels.** Class 6's 65 are
sections of chapters; Classes 9-12's are topics listed under a syllabus
unit. Calling both "topics" flattened two different things into one
word, which is how the contradiction survived.

## 4. What changed in the helpers

- `officialUnitCount()` returns **null** unless `topLevel === 'unit'`.
- `officialSectionCount()` is new: sections, where the top level is the
  chapter.
- `officialTopicCount()` returns null where the top level is the
  chapter.
- `officialTopLevelCount()` is new, and is the honest answer to the
  question the completeness audit actually asks — *does the registry
  hold the structure* — which is about presence, not terminology. The
  audit had been using `officialUnitCount` for this, which worked by
  accident and broke the moment that function was corrected.

`recordsRepresented` is now measured against top-level records rather
than units, for the same reason.
